# Full Body Region And Paediatric Condition Taxonomy

This is a content planning taxonomy, not clinical guidance. Treatment detail should be authored and reviewed separately before publication.

Status labels:

- Active/current: represented in the current pilot site.
- Future: planned for later whole-body expansion.

## Cervical Spine

Status: active/current.

Common conditions:

- Mechanical neck pain.
- Cervical radiculopathy.
- Cervical myelopathy.
- Whiplash-associated disorder.
- Cervical artery dysfunction screening.
- Headache associated with cervical presentations.

Serious pathology and red flags:

- Myelopathy.
- Cervical fracture.
- Infection.
- Malignancy.
- Vascular presentation.
- Progressive neurological deficit.

Differential themes:

- Neck source vs shoulder source.
- Radicular vs peripheral nerve.
- Mechanical pain vs inflammatory/systemic presentation.
- Headache classification and vascular caution.

Reasoning traps:

- Treating arm symptoms as local shoulder pain without neurological screen.
- Missing bilateral or gait symptoms.
- Over-relying on one provocative test.

Case-study ideas:

- Neck and arm symptoms with neurological pattern.
- Hand clumsiness initially referred as peripheral entrapment.
- Post-whiplash dizziness requiring careful screening.

## Thoracic Spine And Rib Cage

Status: active/current for region, early case coverage.

Common conditions:

- Thoracic mechanical pain.
- Rib pain and costovertebral irritation.
- Postural or load-related thoracic pain.
- Thoracic referral patterns.

Serious pathology and red flags:

- Cardiac referral.
- Pulmonary pathology.
- Fracture.
- Infection.
- Malignancy.
- Inflammatory spinal pain.

Differential themes:

- Mechanical thoracic pain vs visceral referral.
- Rib cage pain vs shoulder/cervical referral.
- Load-related pain vs exertional cardiopulmonary symptoms.

Reasoning traps:

- Accepting a plausible posture explanation too early.
- Missing exertional symptoms.
- Treating night or systemic symptoms as routine MSK pain.

Case-study ideas:

- Thoracic/scapular discomfort with cardiac referral concern.
- Rib pain after cough or trauma.
- Persistent thoracic pain with systemic screening cues.

## Shoulder

Status: active/current.

Common conditions:

- Rotator cuff related shoulder pain.
- Adhesive capsulitis.
- Rotator cuff tear.
- Shoulder instability.
- Acromioclavicular joint pain.
- Glenohumeral osteoarthritis.

Serious pathology and red flags:

- Fracture or dislocation.
- Infection.
- Malignancy.
- Referred cardiopulmonary pain.
- Neurological referral from cervical spine.

Differential themes:

- Contractile vs capsular pattern.
- Local shoulder vs cervical referral.
- Traumatic tear vs pain inhibition.
- Stiffness-dominant vs pain-dominant presentation.

Reasoning traps:

- Calling all lateral shoulder pain impingement.
- Missing passive range restriction.
- Missing cervical contribution.

Case-study ideas:

- Lateral shoulder pain with overhead activity.
- Progressive stiffness with diabetes risk factor.
- Acute weakness after traumatic load.

## Wrist And Hand

Status: active/current for region, draft/private case protection present.

- De Quervain tenosynovitis.
- Scaphoid fracture.
- Thumb CMC osteoarthritis.
- Trigger finger.
- Ganglion.
- Tendinopathy and ligament sprain.

Serious pathology and red flags:

- Occult fracture.
- Infection.
- Compartment or vascular compromise.
- Progressive neurological deficit.
- Inflammatory arthritis.

Differential themes:

- Fracture vs sprain.
- Local entrapment vs cervical/peripheral nerve.

Reasoning traps:

- Underestimating hand function impact.

Case-study ideas:

- Wrist pain after fall with normal initial x-ray.
- Thumb-base pain and grip limitation.
- Night paraesthesia with work and systemic risk factors.

## Persistent Pain

Status: future.

Common presentations:

- Persistent low back pain.
- Widespread pain.
- Complex regional pain syndrome awareness.
- Pain-related fear and avoidance.
- Flare management and pacing needs.

Serious pathology and red flags:

- New red flags still require screening.
- Safeguarding or mental health risk where relevant.
- Medication or substance risk where relevant.

Differential themes:

- Nociceptive, neuropathic, and nociplastic contributors.
- Pain sensitivity vs tissue damage.
- Function, confidence, and context.

Reasoning traps:

- Dismissing symptoms as "just chronic pain".
- Missing new pathology in a person with persistent pain.
- Over-medicalising normal flare patterns.

Case-study ideas:

- Persistent back pain with fear avoidance.
- Widespread pain and work impact.
- Flare after activity increase.

## Paediatrics

Status: future.

Paediatric content requires specific expertise, safeguarding awareness, and careful review before publication.

### Screening Principles

Planning themes:

- Developmental stage matters.
- Pain reports may be non-specific.
- Parent/carer history is important but should not replace child-centred assessment.
- Growth plates and apophyses change injury patterns.
- Limping child and night pain need careful reasoning.
- Safeguarding and non-accidental injury awareness must be built into case design.

### Growing Pains Vs Pathology

Differential themes:

- Bilateral intermittent limb pains vs focal progressive pain.
- Normal activity between episodes vs functional decline.
- No systemic symptoms vs fever, weight loss, night sweats, malaise.

Reasoning traps:

- Labelling focal or progressive pain as growing pains.
- Missing systemic or tumour/infection features.

### Limping Child Differential

Planning conditions:

- Transient synovitis.
- Septic arthritis.
- Perthes disease.
- SUFE/SCFE.
- Fracture or stress injury.
- Infection.
- Inflammatory disease.
- Tumour.

Reasoning traps:

- Missing hip pathology presenting as knee pain.
- Missing fever or inability to weight bear.
- Underestimating urgency.

### Hip

Conditions:

- Developmental dysplasia of the hip.
- SUFE/SCFE.
- Perthes disease.
- Transient synovitis.
- Septic arthritis.

Case ideas:

- Limping adolescent with knee pain but hip source.
- Younger child with acute limp and infection screen.

### Knee

Conditions:

- Osgood-Schlatter disease.
- Sinding-Larsen-Johansson syndrome.
- Patellar instability.
- Osteochondritis dissecans.
- Meniscal or ligament injury.

Case ideas:

- Sport-related anterior knee pain and load management.
- Swelling/locking after sport injury.

### Foot And Ankle

Conditions:

- Sever's disease.
- Tarsal coalition.
- Flexible flat foot.
- Clubfoot context.
- Stress injuries.

Case ideas:

- Heel pain in growing athlete.
- Rigid painful flat foot vs flexible flat foot.

### Spine

Conditions:

- Scoliosis.
- Spondylolysis.
- Scheuermann's disease.
- Infection or tumour red flag awareness.

Case ideas:

- Adolescent athlete with extension-related back pain.
- Visible spinal asymmetry noticed by parent.

### Upper Limb

Conditions:

- Nursemaid's elbow.
- Little League elbow/shoulder.
- Physeal stress injuries.
- Fracture and non-accidental injury awareness.

Case ideas:

- Throwing athlete with medial elbow pain.
- Young child with arm disuse after traction mechanism.

### Inflammatory, Infection, Tumour, And Safeguarding Red Flags

Planning red flags:

- Fever.
- Systemic malaise.
- Night sweats.
- Unexplained weight loss.
- Constant or worsening night pain.
- Refusal to weight bear.
- Unexplained bruising.
- Inconsistent mechanism.
- Developmental regression.

### Youth Sport And Load Management

Planning themes:

- Growth and maturation.
- Training spikes.
- Multi-sport load.
- Relative rest vs complete rest.
- Communication with parent, coach, and school where appropriate.

Treatment claims should remain general until reviewed by paediatric specialists.
