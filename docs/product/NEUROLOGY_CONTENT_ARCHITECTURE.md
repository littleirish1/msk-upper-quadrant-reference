# Neurology Content Architecture

Neurology is a planned whole-platform domain, not a completed public curriculum. New material remains private until sources and clinician review are recorded.

## Taxonomy

- Central neurological presentations: stroke/CVA, spinal cord injury, demyelinating and neurodegenerative presentations.
- Peripheral neurological presentations: nerve-root and peripheral-nerve patterns.
- Examination foundations: cranial nerves, dermatomes, myotomes, reflexes, coordination, gait, vestibular screening, and spinal tracts.
- Anatomy foundations: brain regions, spinal cord, tracts, nerve roots, peripheral nerves, and relevant blood vessels.

## Content Types

- Foundation anatomy records.
- Assessment pathways and safety screens.
- Condition/reference pages after approval.
- Guided cases with staged disclosure.
- Outcome measures with licence and measurement-property review.
- OSCE, viva, flashcard, and quiz items derived only from approved content.

## Review Model

Every clinical claim requires source traceability and clinician sign-off. Red flags, referral pathways, lesion localisation, bedside tests, prognosis, and management require explicit review. Draft records remain private and are excluded from search and routes.

The public anatomy index and category routes are available now. The dynamic anatomy detail route is held until at least one record meets the published, reviewed, and public-eligibility contract; this avoids exporting an empty or placeholder clinical page.

## Cranial Nerve Template

The shared anatomy schema supports nerve number, modality, nuclei/origin overview, course, functions, bedside testing, abnormal findings, lesion localisation, cautions, related content, references, and review state. One private metadata-only record validates the schema; it is not clinical teaching content.
