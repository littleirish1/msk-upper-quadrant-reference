# Stakeholder, IP, And Commercialisation Plan

This is planning guidance only. Intellectual property, commercial, regulatory, financial, fundraising, and IPO matters require qualified professional advice before decisions are made.

## Stakeholder Presentation Narrative

### Problem

Physiotherapy learners need more than condition summaries. They need repeated practice making decisions under uncertainty: what to ask, what to examine, what to rule out, when to refer, and how to explain reasoning.

Static resources are useful but often reveal the answer too early. They can support knowledge recall without developing the reasoning process used in clinical practice.

### Proposed Direction

The MSK Clinical Reasoning Lab provides interactive, staged cases that hide the diagnosis until learners commit to a hypothesis. It can show red herrings, safety pivots, and comparison prompts so learners practise the thinking process, not just the final answer.

### Governance Story

Clinical content should remain reviewed before public release. Local AI and source tools can help draft, organise, and compare material, but they should not directly publish clinical advice. Source metadata, route checks, hygiene checks, and human review protect the public learner experience.

### Trust-approved AI pathway: Microsoft Copilot-first option

The Trust currently has an agreed Microsoft Copilot licence. Stakeholder conversations should therefore position Microsoft Copilot, Copilot Studio, and the Trust-approved Microsoft 365 environment as the first institutional AI pathway to explore before external OpenAI, LangChain, or public chatbot integrations.

This is planning guidance only. Trust information governance, IT/security, clinical safety, procurement, and legal review are required before any AI integration.

Why this matters for stakeholders:

- It aligns AI exploration with an existing Trust-approved technology direction.
- It may reduce dependence on unapproved external AI providers during early pilots.
- It may fit better with Microsoft 365, Entra ID, SharePoint, Teams, Purview, and audit arrangements.
- It keeps the product story review-first: AI drafts, humans approve, checks protect publication.

Possible pathway:

1. Static public learner site remains the safest current deployment.
2. Local/back-office `ai-manager` remains private for source and draft workflows.
3. Copilot is used manually, if permitted, for one non-sensitive case-script drafting experiment.
4. Copilot Studio is explored only if custom agents and approved source grounding are allowed.
5. External RAG/vector architecture is considered only later if Trust approval and need are clear.

## Stakeholder Groups

Potential stakeholders:

- Universities.
- NHS education leads.
- Private clinic chains.
- CPD providers.
- Students.
- Newly qualified clinicians.
- Specialist educators.
- Potential investors.
- Professional bodies.

Each group needs a different narrative:

- Students need practice, confidence, and exam/placement preparation.
- Educators need editable, source-tracked case pathways.
- Clinics need onboarding, consistency, and safety escalation training.
- Institutions need governance, data protection, and scalable delivery.
- Investors need market clarity, defensibility, traction, and risk management.

## IP Protection Planning

Likely assets may include:

- Codebase.
- UI/UX design.
- Case structure.
- Authored content.
- Prompts and workflows.
- Source registry model.
- Conversation case engine.
- Brand, name, and logo.
- Datasets and source mappings.
- Generated case templates.
- Review workflows.

Possible protections to explore with professionals:

- Copyright for code, written content, templates, and original educational material.
- Trade marks for brand names, logos, and product identity.
- Trade secrets and confidential know-how for internal workflows, prompts, mappings, and source pipelines.
- Contributor agreements so ownership and licensing are clear.
- Licences for any reused or imported content.
- Terms of use for learners, educators, and institutions.
- Source provenance records for imported materials.
- Repository access controls for private/admin work.
- Brand and domain protection.

Formal IP advice is needed before commercial use, investor conversations, licensing, or institutional pilots.

## Commercialisation Pathway

Possible staged pathway:

1. Pilot demo.
2. Educator and learner feedback.
3. Private beta with a small reviewed content set.
4. Institutional pilot.
5. Subscription or SaaS options.
6. Licensing to universities or clinics.
7. CPD course wrapper.
8. Enterprise or local deployment.
9. Future marketplace of reviewed case packs.

Commercial readiness should be assessed only after evidence of learning value, usability, content quality, review governance, and customer demand.

## IPO Or Shareholder Pathway

An IPO is a much later-stage possibility and should not be presented as near-term readiness.

Prerequisites would likely include:

- Incorporated company.
- Clean cap table.
- Assigned IP ownership.
- Governance structure.
- Accounts and financial controls.
- Data protection processes.
- Product traction.
- Recurring revenue.
- Legal and compliance review.
- Clinical safety governance.
- Scalable infrastructure.
- Investor deck.
- Financial model.
- Risk register.

Before any external investment or shareholder structure, the project would need legal, accounting, tax, data protection, and commercial advice.

## Compliance And Risk Watchlist

Key areas to monitor:

- Medical device and clinical decision support boundary.
- UK GDPR and user data protection.
- Data involving children or paediatrics if minors are represented.
- Copyright permissions for imported teaching materials, papers, slides, and images.
- Professional liability and educational disclaimers.
- Content review governance.
- AI output governance.
- Trust Copilot licence scope, approved data locations, prompt/output retention, audit logging, and custom agent permissions.
- Accessibility requirements.
- Institutional procurement requirements.

## Early Stakeholder Questions

Useful questions for early conversations:

- Which learning moments are hardest to teach using current resources?
- Where do students or new clinicians commonly jump to the wrong diagnosis?
- Which red flags are under-recognised?
- What content would educators want to edit locally?
- What governance would be required before institutional use?
- What evidence would justify a paid pilot?
