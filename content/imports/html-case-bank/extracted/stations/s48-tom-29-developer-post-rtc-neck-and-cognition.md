## Scenario history

**Tom, 29, software developer.** Rear-end RTC 2 weeks ago. Neck pain and stiffness 7/10. Also reports: difficulty concentrating at work since the accident, nausea when looking at screens for >20 minutes, dizziness in supermarkets and bright environments, headache behind eyes. Sleep poor. Anxious about being "damaged." No arm pain. No loss of consciousness at time of incident.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Cervical AROM: globally restricted, pain-limited. No dermatomal sensory loss. Reflexes: normal. VBI screen: negative for vascular features. Smooth pursuit test: mild impairment. Oculomotor exam: difficulty with sustained gaze. Balance: mildly unsteady on tandem gait. No UMN signs.

## Safety flags / red flags

Cognitive symptoms (concentration difficulty), screen sensitivity/nausea, visual-vestibular mismatch (supermarket dizziness), oculomotor impairment = concussion component alongside WAD. Both diagnoses co-exist.

## Legacy diagnosis

WAD Grade II with Concurrent Mild Traumatic Brain Injury (Concussion) — Dual Pathway Required

## Legacy reasoning

WAD: RTC mechanism + cervical restriction + musculoskeletal signs. Concurrent mTBI: cognitive difficulty + screen sensitivity + oculomotor impairment + visual-vestibular mismatch dizziness. These are distinct diagnoses that share symptoms (headache, dizziness, sleep disruption) but require different management approaches. A cervical-only plan misses the concussion; a concussion-only plan misses the cervical pathology.

## Clinical traps

Treating this as WAD only and prescribing a standard cervical exercise programme without addressing the concussion component. Screen-sensitive nausea and oculomotor impairment are NOT cervical symptoms — they require concussion-specific management. Outstanding answer recognises both and designs an integrated plan.

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Whiplash Associated Disorder (WAD) with concurrent mild traumatic brain injury (mTBI/concussion).**

These conditions frequently co-occur in RTC. The clinical challenge: they are distinct diagnoses that share some symptoms (headache, dizziness, cognitive difficulty) but require different management approaches. A purely cervicogenic management plan misses the concussion component; a purely concussion plan misses the cervical pathology.

### 2. WAD vs Concussion Feature Map

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

- **WAD features:** Neck pain/stiffness, restricted cervical ROM, headache from upper neck, psychosocial yellow flags
- **Concussion features:** Cognitive symptoms (difficulty concentrating, memory problems), visual disturbance, oculomotor dysfunction, balance disturbance, fatigue, emotional lability, photosensitivity, screen sensitivity, nausea with visual stimuli
- **Overlap:** Headache (both), dizziness (both), sleep disturbance (both) — these overlapping symptoms can mask the concussion component in a patient presenting primarily with neck pain

### 3. Concussion Assessment — What Cannot Be Missed

- Icon: 🔴
- Badge: db-safety

**Concussion Red Flags — Emergency Referral**

- Worsening headache beyond initial 48 hours
- Repeated vomiting
- Seizure
- Focal neurological deficit
- Declining consciousness
- Suspected skull fracture

Screen for concussion using SCAT5 or equivalent. If screen positive: concussion-specific management pathway — return-to-learn before return-to-sport protocol. Vestibular and oculomotor physiotherapy if indicated. Do not prescribe cervical exercise programme in isolation if concussion is active.

### 4. Examination — Dual Screen

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-obj

- Cervical AROM + IFOMPT VBI screen
- Smooth pursuit + oculomotor assessment
- Balance testing: tandem gait, Romberg — cervicogenic vs vestibular dizziness differentiation
- SCAT5 cognitive screen if not already completed
- King-Devick test if available (saccadic eye movements)

### 5. Management — Integrated Plan

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-mgmt

- Phase 1: Relative cognitive and physical rest — light activity only if symptoms allow
- Concussion pathway: return-to-learn → return-to-sport protocol (symptom-guided)
- Cervical component: graded ROM, sensorimotor retraining, gentle manual therapy once cognitive load tolerates
- Vestibular rehab: if oculomotor or balance involvement confirmed
- Psychosocial: education, anxiety management, expectation setting

### 6. Evidence

- Icon: 🩷
- Badge: db-evid

WAD and mTBI frequently co-occur in RTC. The outstanding advanced practice clinician recognises both and does not treat one at the expense of the other. Concussion management: symptom-guided return-to-activity protocol (Consensus Statement on Concussion in Sport — Berlin 2016). WAD: OPTIMa Côté 2017; Walton & Elliott 2017 — heterogeneity of WAD. The dual pathway approach reflects genuine advanced clinical reasoning.
