## Scenario history

**Brendan, 45, self-employed plasterer.** 11-month right lateral elbow pain, initially intermittent/infrequent, now painful every day in work and at any other time. Reports good response to steroid injection 4 months ago performed by GP — relief lasted 3 weeks. USS by upper limb consultant: "small intra-tendinous tear, ongoing inflammatory tendon insertion response." Consultant suggested another injection or surgery.

Presents to your clinic — wants referred for injection / would like you to carry out a steroid injection.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Localised tenderness CEO right elbow. Positive tennis elbow cluster: Cozen's strongly positive, Maudsley's positive. AROM full. Grip strength reduced right vs left. Neurodynamic: mildly sensitised radial bias ULTT. Cervical screen negative. PRTEE score: 68/100.

## Safety flags / red flags

No red flags. No fracture. No systemic features. USS has already been performed — no complete tear.

## Legacy diagnosis

Lateral Epicondylitis — Injection Dilemma

## Legacy reasoning

ECRB tendinopathy confirmed clinically and by USS. NICE (Oct 2025) and BESS 2023 recommend against routine CSI — worse outcomes at 6 months vs exercise. The clinical challenge is navigating the patient's reasonable expectation for injection while providing evidence-based care. Outstanding answer: acknowledge prior response, explain the evidence timeline, propose structured 4-phase rehabilitation, discuss USS findings as supportive of active loading approach.

## Clinical traps

Simply refusing the injection without a structured alternative. A good answer says 'the evidence doesn't support repeat CSI'. An outstanding answer acknowledges the patient's reasonable expectation (the first injection worked short-term), explains the NICE Oct 2025 evidence timeline (worse outcomes at 6 months), uses the USS findings as positive evidence for active tendon loading, and proposes the BESS 2023 4-phase rehabilitation programme as the evidence-based alternative — making the patient feel heard, not dismissed.

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Lateral epicondylitis (tennis elbow) — ECRB tendinopathy, ongoing inflammatory insertion response.**

Right-sided lateral elbow pain in a manual worker, aggravated by wrist extension and grip tasks, with localised CEO tenderness, positive tennis elbow cluster, and USS confirming tendon pathology. The patient has already had a corticosteroid injection 4 months ago with only short-lived benefit.

### 2. Differential Diagnoses

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

- **Posterior interosseous nerve entrapment / radial tunnel syndrome** — pain more distal, over the arcade of Frohse; reproduced by resisted supination not wrist extension
- **Cervical radiculopathy (C6)** — if symptoms extend above elbow and neck movement reproduces or modifies pain
- **Elbow joint OA** — older patients, movement restriction, crepitus
- **Referred pain** — TOS, cervical, shoulder pathology

### 3. Objective Assessment

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-obj

- Active and passive ROM — flexion, extension, supination, pronation
- Resisted wrist extension (aggravates), resisted middle finger extension (Maudsley's)
- **Tennis elbow cluster:** Cozen's (resisted wrist extension), Mill's (neural stretch/wrist flex + elbow extension), Maudsley's
- Palpation: CEO, radial tunnel (4cm distal)
- Neurodynamic: ULTT2 radial bias (differentiate PIN entrapment)
- Cervical screen
- DASH / PRTEE outcome measures

### 4. Red Flags / Safety

- Icon: 🔴
- Badge: db-safety

**Screen For**

- Significant swelling or haemarthrosis
- Suspicion of fracture
- Systemic features (multiple joints, RA, gout)
- Suspected septic joint
- Malignancy

USS findings of small intra-tendinous tear require careful management — not a contraindication to exercise but should guide loading approach.

### 5. Management

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-mgmt

**The steroid injection question — outstanding-level reasoning required.**

- NICE (Oct 2025): do NOT routinely offer corticosteroid injection — worse outcomes at 6 months
- BESS 2023: 4-phase rehabilitation is the evidence-based pathway
- Phase 1: offload / relative rest from aggravating activities
- Phase 2: reload / adapt
- Phase 3: progressive eccentric and isotonic loading — kinetic chain
- Phase 4: return to full activity
- Topical NSAIDs, simple analgesia as adjuncts
- Monitor with PRTEE at baseline and review

**✦ On the injection request**

Acknowledge the patient's request and previous short-term benefit. Explain the evidence: short-term pain relief confirmed, but multiple RCTs show outcomes at 6 and 12 months are worse in the injection group vs exercise. Explore readiness to engage with a structured loading programme. Discuss USS findings as supportive of active tendon loading approach.

### 6. Communication

- Icon: 🟣
- Badge: db-comm

**Outstanding Model Language**

"I completely understand why you'd want another injection — the relief you got last time was real. However, the current evidence suggests that while injections do help pain in the short term, they actually seem to lead to worse outcomes over the following months compared to a structured exercise programme. The scan shows your tendon is still reacting — which is actually good news, because that means it will respond to the right loading approach. I'd like to talk you through what that would look like."

### 7. Evidence Base

- Icon: 🩷
- Badge: db-evid

**Key Evidence**

- **NICE (Revised Oct 2025)** — Do not offer CSI routinely; discuss adverse effects and long-term implications
- **BESS 2023 patient care pathway: Tennis elbow** (Singh et al.) — Structured stepped rehabilitation pathway; eccentric loading; multi-disciplinary
- **Lapner P et al, J Shoulder Elbow Surgery (2021)** — Nonoperative treatment of lateral epicondylitis: SR + MA — supports exercise over injection long-term
- **Lucado et al. 2022, JOSPT APTA/HJUEPT CPG** — Comprehensive lateral elbow tendinopathy guidelines

**⭐ Outstanding — Nuanced Appraisal**

The evidence base for lateral epicondylitis shows a consistent pattern: CSI provides better short-term pain relief but exercise is superior at 12 months. An outstanding answer names this timeline, cites the evidence, and uses it to genuinely inform shared decision-making rather than simply refusing the patient's request.
