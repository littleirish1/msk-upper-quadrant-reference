## Scenario history

**Mark, 49.** Sudden pain and "pop" in antecubital fossa lifting a heavy box yesterday. Bruising developing over the past 24 hours. Weakness with supination and elbow flexion. Pain 5/10 at rest, 8/10 with lifting or supination. Wants to know if he needs urgent onward referral.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Observation: ecchymosis over antecubital fossa, mild visible soft tissue asymmetry. Palpation: tenderness in antecubital fossa, possible gap at distal biceps insertion. AROM: elbow flexion full but painful; supination significantly restricted and weak. O'Driscoll Hook test: examiner unable to hook cord-like structure under biceps tendon from lateral side — positive result (complete rupture likely). X-ray: no fracture.

## Safety flags / red flags

No neurovascular compromise at rest. Time-critical surgical decision: complete distal biceps rupture ideally repaired within 2-3 weeks.

## Legacy diagnosis

Distal Biceps Tendon Rupture — Complete (likely)

## Legacy reasoning

Sudden mechanism + "pop" + antecubital fossa bruising + supination weakness + positive hook test (no cord to hook) = complete distal biceps rupture. Supination weakness is the KEY functional indicator — the biceps is the primary supinator; significant supination weakness after this mechanism = biceps rupture until proven otherwise. Time-critical: 2-3 week window for primary repair before retraction and fibrosis.

## Clinical traps

Treating as a sprain or contusion and not recognising the time-critical surgical window. The combination of sudden mechanism + "pop" + supination weakness is pathognomonic for distal biceps rupture. Not performing (or not interpreting correctly) the Hook test. Lukkaala 2020: negative hook test does NOT exclude rupture — outstanding answer includes this caveat.

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Distal biceps tendon rupture.**

Three features: (1) sudden onset "pop" in antecubital fossa lifting heavy load = classic mechanism of distal biceps rupture; (2) bruising + weakness specifically with supination and elbow flexion = functional deficit matching distal biceps anatomy; (3) acute onset yesterday = time-critical surgical decision window is open.

### 2. Safety — Urgent Referral Decision

- Icon: 🔴
- Badge: db-safety

**Yes — Urgent Onward Referral Required**

Distal biceps complete rupture requires surgical repair ideally within 2-3 weeks for optimal outcome. Retraction and fibrosis after this window makes repair technically more difficult with less predictable results.

Refer urgently to upper limb orthopaedic surgery. Immobilise pending review. Advise against heavy lifting or supination loading while awaiting referral.

### 3. Communication

- Icon: 🟣
- Badge: db-comm

**Explaining Urgency**

"What you've described — the pop and the weakness you have now with turning your forearm — is consistent with a tendon in the front of your elbow having ruptured. This needs to be seen by an orthopaedic surgeon quickly, because the window for the best surgical outcome is the next couple of weeks. I'm going to refer you urgently today. In the meantime, please avoid heavy lifting or anything that requires forceful twisting of your forearm."

### 4. Evidence

- Icon: 🩷
- Badge: db-evid

Surgical repair within 2-3 weeks of distal biceps complete rupture is associated with better outcomes; delayed repair is technically more demanding. Lukkaala et al. (2020): Hook test useful for establishing diagnosis but negative test does NOT exclude rupture.
