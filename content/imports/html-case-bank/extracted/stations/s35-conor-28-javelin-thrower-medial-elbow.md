## Scenario history

**Conor, 28, competitive javelin thrower.** 6-week history of medial right elbow pain during and after throwing. Pain 4/10 during warm-up, 7/10 at full effort. Some ulnar border forearm ache. No locking or giving way. No significant trauma. Regional championships in 8 weeks.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Medial epicondyle tenderness, especially common flexor origin. Resisted wrist flexion: pain at medial epicondyle. Valgus stress test (20-30° flexion): pain medially but no laxity — CC intact. Tinel's at cubital tunnel: mildly positive. Mild ring/little finger tingling. Grip strength: mildly reduced. Cervical screen: negative.

## Safety flags / red flags

UCL laxity not detected. Mild ulnar nerve sensitivity co-exists. No fracture red flags.

## Legacy diagnosis

Medial Epicondylitis with Mild Ulnar Neuropathy — Throwing Athlete

## Legacy reasoning

Common flexor origin tenderness + resisted wrist flexion pain + overhead throwing mechanism = medial epicondylitis. Valgus stress: pain but NO laxity = tendon involvement not UCL tear. Mild cubital tunnel sensitivity = co-existing ulnar nerve irritation from repeated valgus loading. Outstanding answer distinguishes pain from laxity in the valgus stress test.

## Clinical traps

Misinterpreting valgus stress test pain as UCL instability. Pain alone ≠ UCL tear — laxity with soft end-feel is required for UCL instability diagnosis. Also: missing the ulnar nerve component — cubital tunnel sensitivity is common in throwing athletes and requires activity modification.

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Medial epicondylitis (golfer's elbow) — with UCL involvement requiring screening.**

Common flexor origin tendinopathy at medial epicondyle. 7× less common than lateral epicondylitis (Cutts 2019). Medial elbow pain in a throwing athlete — must consider UCL pathology as it shares clinical territory with medial epicondylitis and has very different management implications.

### 2. Key Differentials + Discriminator

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

- **UCL injury:** Valgus stress instability, throwing mechanism, ulnar nerve symptoms. Discriminator: valgus stress test at 20-30° — laxity vs pain only.
- **Cubital tunnel syndrome:** Ulnar nerve — ring/little finger paraesthesia, Tinel's at cubital tunnel. Discriminator: sensory symptoms + cubital tunnel cluster.
- **Cervical C8-T1 radiculopathy:** Neck provocation, dermatomal pattern. Discriminator: cervical AROM reproduces medial arm symptoms.

### 3. Key Tests

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-obj

- **Resisted wrist flexion:** Reproduces medial epicondyle pain = common flexor origin involvement.
- **Valgus stress test (20-30° elbow flexion):** _Apply valgus force medially. Pain alone ≠ UCL instability. Laxity + pain = UCL involvement. Critical distinction for throwing athletes._
- **Cubital tunnel cluster:** Flexion compression, Tinel's, Froment's — screen for ulnar neuropathy which commonly co-exists.

### 4. Management

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-mgmt

- Education, load management, activity modification
- Progressive wrist flexor and forearm strengthening (same 4-phase model as lateral epicondylitis)
- Eccentric loading programme
- If UCL component: may require orthopedic review — Tommy John surgery in throwing athletes with complete UCL tears
- NSAIDs topical; CSI may be considered but same caveats as lateral epicondylitis apply

### 5. Evidence

- Icon: 🩷
- Badge: db-evid

Medial epicondylitis: same pathophysiology as lateral (tendon changes + motor control impairments + possible central sensitisation — Lucado 2022). The UCL distinction is the clinically critical point in this station — throwing athletes with UCL involvement have very different management pathways. Outstanding answer identifies valgus laxity (not just pain) as the discriminating finding.
