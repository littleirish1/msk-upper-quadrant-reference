## Scenario history

**Orlagh, 34, desk-based role and swims recreationally.** Three-month history of intermittent paraesthesia and dull ache from supraclavicular fossa into ulnar border forearm and hand. Worse with prolonged keyboard use, driving, and swimming front crawl. Improved by arm support and shoulder girdle relaxation. Mild neck ache only, no clear neck provocation. Reports occasional transient hand colour change during swimming but normal colour at rest. No trauma. Previous cervical X-ray reported as normal.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Cervical AROM: full, pain-free, no arm symptoms reproduced. Neurological screen: normal power, reflexes, dermatomal sensation. Radial pulse: present bilaterally at rest, no colour change, normal skin temperature. EAST test (3 min): symptom reproduction at 2 minutes — arm heaviness and ulnar paraesthesia reproduced. Wright's: symptom reproduction + mild pulse diminution. Costoclavicular compression: positive — familiar symptoms reproduced. Scalene triangle palpation: mild tenderness right.

## Safety flags / red flags

No vascular features at rest. Transient colour change during swimming — must be clarified before proceeding. No Horner's syndrome. No constitutional features.

## Legacy diagnosis

Neurogenic TOS (nTOS) — most consistent diagnosis

## Legacy reasoning

C8-T1 distribution WITHOUT cervical provocation + overhead/sustained posture provocation + positive TOS cluster + prior normal cervical investigations = nTOS (Illig 2016: 3 of 4 criteria met — local signs, nerve compression signs, absence of other pathology). The transient colour change during swimming requires vascular screening before any provocative testing — if features suggest aTOS, urgent vascular referral takes precedence.

## Clinical traps

Performing provocative TOS testing before screening for vascular features. The colour change during swimming must be clarified FIRST — if vascular features are present, management changes completely. Also: accepting normal NCS as ruling out TOS. NCS can be normal in nTOS because compression is dynamic and intermittent. A good answer diagnoses nTOS; an outstanding answer cites Illig 2016 criteria and explains why normal NCS doesn't exclude it.

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Neurogenic TOS (nTOS).**

Three features: (1) ulnar border paraesthesia with overhead and sustained posture provocation = C8-T1 distribution linked to thoracic outlet loading; (2) relief with arm support and shoulder girdle relaxation = load-dependent mechanism; (3) no neck provocation + normal cervical X-ray = absence of other pathology (Illig 2016 criterion 3).

### 2. Key Differentials + Discriminator

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

- **C8-T1 radiculopathy:** Neck provocation reproduces concordant symptoms; dermatomal pattern; positive Spurling. _Against here:_ no neck provocation, normal cervical X-ray.
- **Ulnar neuropathy at cubital tunnel:** Provoked by elbow flexion; Tinel's at cubital tunnel; less overhead-dependent. _Against here:_ overhead and sustained posture provocation not specifically elbow flexion.
- **Vascular TOS:** Prominent colour change, coldness, swelling, pulse changes. _Must screen — transient colour change during swimming requires vascular screening FIRST before any provocative testing._

### 3. Vascular Safety Screen

- Icon: 🔴
- Badge: db-safety

**⚠ Screen for Vascular Features FIRST**

The transient colour change must be actively clarified before proceeding with provocative TOS testing.

Clarify: persistent or transient? Swelling, coldness, heaviness? Pulse deficit at rest? If significant vascular features: medical/vascular assessment before physiotherapy. Safety net: if colour change worsens or becomes persistent → urgent medical review.

### 4. Two Tests + Interpretive Value

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-obj

- **EAST / Roos test (3 min):** Sustained 90° abduction + ER with repeated hand opening/closing. _Positive = symptom reproduction. Reliability is limited — interpret within clinical context, not diagnostic in isolation._
- **Wright's Hyperabduction:** Passive full arm elevation, monitor radial pulse. _Positive = symptom reproduction AND pulse volume change. Symptom alone is NOT positive — must include pulse assessment._

### 5. High-Quality Management Plan

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-mgmt

- Education on thoracic outlet mechanism and reassurance
- Load management: keyboard ergonomics, driving posture, swimming stroke modification
- Scapular stabilisation: lower/mid trapezius, serratus anterior endurance
- Scalene and pec minor stretching
- Neural mobilisation (cautious, monitor response)
- Graded return to swimming: start backstroke, progress to front crawl
- Symptom monitoring + agreed escalation criteria

### 6. Evidence &amp; Communication

- Icon: 🩷
- Badge: db-evid

**Evidence**

- **Illig KA et al. (2016)** — nTOS diagnostic criteria: 3 of 4; scalene block most objective criterion
- **STOPNTOS Trial** — Surgery offers short-term gains for non-responders to physiotherapy — defines escalation pathway

**Communication**

"Before I do any tests on your arm positions today, I need to check on that colour change you mentioned during swimming — that's important to clarify first. If that's related to blood flow rather than nerve compression, we need to handle that differently. For the nerve symptoms, what you're experiencing appears to be related to the area just above your collarbone where nerves pass on their way to your arm — this usually responds well to physiotherapy."
