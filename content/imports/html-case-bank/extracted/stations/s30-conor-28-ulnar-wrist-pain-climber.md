## Scenario history

**Conor, 28, competitive rock climber.** Ulnar-sided wrist pain 4–7/10 with loading through wrist in extension and ulnar deviation. Clicking with rotation. Pain with push-up position. Insidious onset over past 2 months, no specific trauma. Wants graded return to climbing in six weeks.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

AROM: full wrist ROM; mildly painful at end-range extension + ulnar deviation. Palpation: tenderness over TFCC region (ulnar fovea). TFCC compression test: positive — localised ulnar-sided pain with axial compression + ulnar deviation. Fovea sign: mildly positive — tenderness in fovea region. DRUJ: stable to piano key test. No radial-sided tenderness. Grip strength: mildly reduced right.

## Safety flags / red flags

No complete TFCC tear signs (DRUJ stable, Fovea sign mildly not strongly positive). No avascular necrosis suspected. Manageable for conservative pathway initially.

## Legacy diagnosis

TFCC Irritation/Partial Tear — Load-Related

## Legacy reasoning

Ulnar-sided wrist pain + extension/deviation loading mechanism (climbing) + clicking with rotation + positive TFCC compression test + positive Fovea sign = TFCC involvement. Climbing-specific mechanism: compression loading through wrist in extension and deviation is a recognised TFCC loading position. DRUJ stability intact and Fovea sign mild = suggests irritation/partial rather than complete tear.

## Clinical traps

Not differentiating TFCC from ECU tendinopathy — both cause ulnar-sided wrist pain. ECU tendinopathy: localised ECU groove pain, provoked by resisted ulnar deviation/wrist extension specifically, ECU tendon palpation reproduces symptoms. Outstanding answer demonstrates palpation of ECU groove vs fovea sign specifically. Also: promising 6 weeks without staging the rehabilitation clearly — the outstanding answer phases the return to climbing with specific criteria.

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**TFCC irritation/tear.**

Three features: (1) ulnar-sided wrist pain with wrist loading in extension and deviation = TFCC loading mechanism; (2) clicking with rotation = possible TFCC structural component; (3) pain with push-up position = compression and rotation loading consistent with TFCC involvement in the ulnar zone of the wrist.

### 2. Key Differentials + Discriminator

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

- **ECU tendinopathy:** Pain along ECU groove, resisted ulnar deviation provocation. _Discriminator:_ palpate ECU groove specifically — localised tenderness vs diffuse ulnar-sided pain.
- **DRUJ instability:** Piano key sign positive, pain with forearm rotation. _Discriminator:_ DRUJ stability assessment — is there vertical instability of the distal ulna?
- **Ulnar impaction syndrome:** Positive-ulnar variance on X-ray, pain with ulnar-loaded wrist. _Against here:_ no X-ray showing ulnar variance, young recreational athlete.

### 3. Safety &amp; Progression

- Icon: 🔴
- Badge: db-safety

Screen for: complete TFCC tear (if Fovea sign strongly positive → consider MRI + surgical review); AVN of carpal bones; significant instability. Progression: if worsening with load or failure to respond at 6-8 weeks → USS or MRI + surgical review.

### 4. Two Tests + Interpretive Value

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-obj

- **TFCC compression test:** Ulnar deviation + axial compression of wrist. _Positive = localised ulnar-sided pain ± clunk/grinding = TFCC involvement._
- **Fovea sign:** Palpation into fovea between FCU and ulna styloid. _Positive = localised deep tenderness = TFCC foveal attachment involvement. Strongly positive = MRI indicated._

### 5. Management + Return to Climbing (6 weeks)

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-mgmt

- Phase 1 (weeks 1-2): Offload TFCC — wrist brace during climbing, avoid loaded extension/deviation
- Phase 2 (weeks 2-4): Graded forearm and grip strengthening, wrist proprioception, progress rotation tolerance
- Phase 3 (weeks 4-6): Progressive climbing load — moderate holds in neutral wrist first, avoid slopers/compression initially, build to full load
- Return criteria: pain ≤2/10 with full training load, grip strength ≥90% contralateral
- Outcome measure: PRWE (Patient Rated Wrist Evaluation)

### 6. Communication

- Icon: 🩷
- Badge: db-evid

**Communication**

"The structure on the ulnar side of your wrist — a cartilage and ligament complex — is irritated by the loading positions climbing demands, especially compression and rotation. Six weeks is an achievable timeline if we're smart about it. We'll start by reducing the irritation, then progressively load your wrist back to full climbing demands. The key is working with your goal, not against it."
