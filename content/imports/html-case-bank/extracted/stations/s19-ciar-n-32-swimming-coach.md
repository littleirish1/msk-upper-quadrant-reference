## Scenario history

**Ciarán, 32, competitive swimming coach.** 6-month right arm heaviness and ulnar border paraesthesia, worst with overhead coaching demonstrations and sustained arm elevation. Intermittent occipital headaches on the same side. Symptoms variable day to day. No significant neck pain. No trauma. Previous investigations (cervical MRI, nerve conduction studies) reported as "unremarkable." Multiple GP visits with no diagnosis. Frustrated.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Cervical AROM: full, pain-free. Neurological screen: normal power, reflexes, sensation in dermatomal distribution. Radial pulse: present bilaterally at rest. EAST test (3 min): symptom reproduction at 90 seconds — arm heaviness, ulnar paraesthesia, occipital headache reproduced. Wright's: symptom reproduction + mild pulse diminution. Costoclavicular compression: positive — familiar symptoms. Palpation: scalene triangle tenderness right. No pallor/cyanosis/swelling at rest.

## Safety flags / red flags

No vascular features at rest. No Horner's. No constitutional features. No cervical radiculopathy pattern.

## Legacy diagnosis

Neurogenic Thoracic Outlet Syndrome (nTOS) — most consistent diagnosis

## Legacy reasoning

C8-T1 distribution WITHOUT cervical provocation + overhead provocation + positive provocative TOS cluster + prior negative investigations = nTOS. Diagnostic criteria (Illig 2016): 3 of 4 required — local signs, nerve compression signs, absence of other pathology (previous investigations negative), and positive response to scalene block (not yet performed). Management: physiotherapy first-line (scalene/pec minor stretching, scapular stabilisers, neural mobilisation, postural retraining).

## Clinical traps

Accepting the 'normal NCS' finding as ruling out TOS. NCS can be normal in nTOS because the compression is dynamic/intermittent and may not produce a fixed conduction deficit. Outstanding answer: NCS normal + symptoms + positive cluster + overhead provocation = clinical diagnosis of nTOS is still possible; the scalene block is the most objective diagnostic criterion (Illig 2016). Also: not performing the vascular screen BEFORE provocative testing.

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Neurogenic Thoracic Outlet Syndrome (nTOS) — disputed presentation.**

A 32-year-old swimming coach presenting with right-sided arm heaviness, ulnar border paraesthesia, and occipital headaches provoked by sustained overhead coaching postures. Intermittent symptoms worst with arm elevation. No vascular features at rest. Cervical movement does not clearly reproduce symptoms. No obvious dermatomal pattern.

### 2. Differential Diagnoses

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

- **C8-T1 cervical radiculopathy** — if neck movement reproduces concordant arm symptoms; dermatomal pattern
- **Cervical myelopathy** — if bilateral, progressive, UMN signs present
- **Ulnar neuropathy at elbow (cubital tunnel)** — if symptoms localise to ulnar border without overhead provocation
- **Carpal tunnel syndrome** — if median nerve distribution
- **CRPS** — if burning, allodynia, trophic changes
- **Pancoast tumour** — if C8-T1 symptoms + constitutional features, Horner's syndrome

### 3. Objective Assessment

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-obj

- **Vascular screen FIRST** — radial pulse, skin colour, swelling at rest
- Cervical AROM + neurological screen (to exclude radiculopathy)
- **Provocative TOS tests (interpret as cluster):**
- Costoclavicular compression test — symptoms + pulse change
- EAST / Roos test — 3 min sustained; symptom reproduction
- Wright's hyperabduction — symptom reproduction + pulse volume change
- ULLT — assess neural mechanosensitivity
- Palpation: scalene triangle, pec minor, first rib

**✦ Critical Note**

No single TOS test is diagnostic. EAST reliability is limited — combine with history and other tests. Wright's positive = symptom reproduction AND pulse change — not just symptoms alone.

### 4. Red Flags / Safety

- Icon: 🔴
- Badge: db-safety

**Vascular Screen is Mandatory**

- Assess for aTOS / vTOS before any provocative testing
- Persistent pallor, cyanosis, absent pulse = urgent vascular referral
- Pancoast: C8-T1 + Horner's syndrome + constitutional features → urgent CT chest

### 5. Management

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-mgmt

- Physiotherapy first-line for nTOS — stretching (scalene, pec minor), scapular stabilisers (lower/mid trapezius), neural mobilisation, postural retraining, ergonomic advice
- Address overhead posture and coaching technique
- SIN(SS) approach — manage irritability first
- Scalene block injection: diagnostic and potentially therapeutic
- EAST x3 daily for 3 days (if positive response >50% reduction = supports conservative pathway)
- Surgery (first rib resection, scalenectomy) if conservative fails — STOPNTOS Trial: short-term functional gains over continued physiotherapy
- Medications: NSAIDs, neuropathic agents as symptom control

### 6. Communication

- Icon: 🟣
- Badge: db-comm

TOS is often a diagnosis of exclusion that has been long-delayed. Patients frequently present frustrated by previous investigations and failed treatments. Acknowledge complexity and uncertainty while offering a structured evidence-informed plan.

**Outstanding Language**

"This type of nerve and muscle tightness around the shoulder and neck region can be genuinely complex and takes time to resolve. I want to be honest with you — the diagnosis takes careful assessment, but the good news is that most people with this presentation do respond well to the right physiotherapy programme focused on your posture and the muscles around your neck and shoulder blade."

### 7. Evidence Base

- Icon: 🩷
- Badge: db-evid

**Key Evidence**

- **STOPNTOS Trial (randomised)** — Surgery vs continued conservative treatment: beneficial effect of surgery for nTOS patients not responding to physiotherapy (short-term)
- **Illig et al. 2016, Reporting Standards, Society for Vascular Surgery** — nTOS diagnostic criteria; EAST protocol
- **Costoclavicular compression test** — compression of neurovascular bundle between clavicle and first rib; patient retracts + depresses scapula; positive if familiar symptoms reproduced ± radial pulse obliteration

**⭐ Outstanding — Controversy**

nTOS remains the most controversial of the three TOS types because: (1) it lacks reliable diagnostic markers; (2) classification between true and disputed nTOS is contested; (3) surgical outcomes are inconsistent. Acknowledging this uncertainty while offering a structured clinical approach is the mark of an advanced practitioner.
