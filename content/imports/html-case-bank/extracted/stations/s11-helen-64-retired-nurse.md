## Scenario history

**Helen, 64, retired nurse.** 2-year history of pain and stiffness at the base of the right thumb. Worse when pinching and gripping — opening jars, writing, turning keys. Morning stiffness lasting 15 minutes. No swelling of other joints. Family history of arthritis. GP told her "it's just wear and tear, nothing can be done."

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Observation: mild squaring deformity at first CMC joint. Palpation: bony tenderness at CMC joint. CMC Grind Test: positive — crepitus + pain. Finkelstein's test: mildly positive but pain is at CMC not radial styloid. Pinch grip strength: reduced. No synovitis. No systemic features.

## Safety flags / red flags

No inflammatory features suggesting RA or psoriatic arthritis. No systemic features.

## Legacy diagnosis

First CMC (Thumb Base) Osteoarthritis

## Legacy reasoning

Classic presentation: post-menopausal woman, insidious onset, CMC joint location, grind test positive, activity-related pain with grip/pinch. Differentiate from De Quervain's (Finkelstein's strongly positive, APL/EPB tenderness, no crepitus) and inflammatory arthritis (systemic features, bilateral, synovitis). GP's nihilism is incorrect — substantial evidence for hand exercise, splinting, and stepped pharmacological management.

## Clinical traps

Accepting the GP's 'nothing can be done' framing without challenging it. The outstanding answer explicitly refutes therapeutic nihilism with evidence — hand OA is manageable, exercise is effective (Ros et al. 2015), and topical NSAIDs are first-line pharmacotherapy. Also: not ruling out de Quervain's — the CMC grind test differentiates (grind = CMC OA; Finkelstein's positive = de Quervain's). CMC pain during Finkelstein's is not the same as a positive de Quervain's test.

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Thumb base / first CMC osteoarthritis.**

Pain at the base of the thumb during pinch and grip tasks, weakness, and reduced function. Symptoms — not imaging severity — should guide management.

### 2. Differential Diagnoses

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

- De Quervain's tenosynovitis (Finkelstein's positive)
- Inflammatory arthritis
- MCP pathology
- Referred neuropathic pain if atypical

### 3. Objective Assessment

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-obj

- Site-specific palpation
- Grip and pinch assessment
- CMC Grind Test
- Observation of deformity or instability
- Functional task assessment

### 4. Management

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-mgmt

**Stepwise approach:**

- Education and supported self-management
- Activity modification / joint protection / offloading
- Hand exercises
- Splinting where appropriate
- Topical NSAIDs — first-line pharmacological strategy
- Corticosteroid injection for persistent symptoms after adequate conservative care
- Surgery (trapeziectomy) if disabling symptoms persist despite appropriate non-surgical treatment

### 5. Communication

- Icon: 🟣
- Badge: db-comm

An excellent answer should directly challenge **therapeutic nihilism** and explain that hand OA is treatable even if not curable.

**Example language**

"Although there is some wear and tear in the joint, there's a lot we can do to manage your symptoms. Exercise and the right support for your thumb can make a real difference to what you're able to do day to day."
