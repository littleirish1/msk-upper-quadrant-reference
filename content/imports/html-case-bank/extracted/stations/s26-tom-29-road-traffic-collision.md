## Scenario history

**Tom, 29.** Rear-end RTC 10 days ago. Neck pain 7/10, worse with rotation and extension. Marked stiffness, reduced active rotation bilaterally. Occipital headache. Dizziness in supermarkets and nausea with scrolling on phone. Sleep poor, highly anxious about damage. No arm pain, no paraesthesia.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Cervical AROM: globally restricted, pain-limited in all directions. No dermatomal sensory loss. Reflexes: normal and symmetric. No UMN signs. ULTT: mildly sensitised bilaterally (non-specific). VBI screen completed — dizziness in supermarkets characterised as visual-vestibular mismatch (visual stimulus dependent, not head-movement dependent in isolation). Negative VBI screen for vascular involvement. Palpation: global cervical tenderness, most marked C2-C5.

## Safety flags / red flags

VBI screen completed and negative. Canadian C-Spine Rule applied — low risk, no imaging required. No progressive neurology. Psychosocial yellow flags: anxiety about damage, fear of driving, sleep disruption, catastrophising.

## Legacy diagnosis

Whiplash Associated Disorder (WAD) — Grade II with significant psychosocial features

## Legacy reasoning

Mechanism consistent with WAD. Grade II: musculoskeletal signs (restricted ROM, tenderness) without neurological deficit. Dizziness likely reflects cervicogenic or vestibulo-ocular disruption (visual-vestibular mismatch in supermarket context) rather than vascular compromise — VBI screen negative. Yellow flags (anxiety, sleep, fear) are as clinically significant as the physical impairment and must be addressed directly.

## Clinical traps

Treating this as purely biomechanical and missing the yellow flags as clinical targets. Fear of driving, sleep disruption, and anxiety about damage are not background noise — they are primary treatment targets. Outstanding answer explicitly names psychosocial factors and explains how management adapts. Also: attributing the dizziness to vascular cause without completing VBI screen, or over-medicalising the dizziness without exploring its triggers.

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Whiplash Associated Disorder (WAD) — Grade II.**

Three features: (1) rear-end RTC mechanism = classic WAD etiology; (2) bilateral restricted cervical rotation with global stiffness = musculoskeletal signs present (Grade II); (3) significant psychosocial yellow flags — anxiety about damage, fear of driving, sleep disruption = require active biopsychosocial management.

### 2. Key Differentials + Discriminator

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

- **Cervical fracture/instability:** Canadian C-Spine Rule applies — if positive criteria, image first. _Against here (provisionally):_ ambulatory, no midline tenderness described — but always apply formal criteria.
- **Mild TBI/concussion:** Nausea with scrolling and supermarket dizziness = vestibulo-ocular symptoms may indicate concussion component. _Important:_ this is not "just WAD" if vestibulo-ocular features are present — needs appropriate screening.
- **Cervical vascular injury:** IFOMPT vascular screen essential given mechanism — 3Ds/3Ns/1A.

### 3. Safety Screen Before Assessment

- Icon: 🔴
- Badge: db-safety

**Screen Before Starting Assessment**

- Canadian C-Spine Rule / NEXUS — fracture risk; if positive: image first
- IFOMPT vascular screen: 3Ds/3Ns/1A — dizziness needs characterising
- Concussion protocol if vestibulo-ocular features confirmed

### 4. Management — Physical &amp; Psychosocial

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-mgmt

- Education and reassurance: reduce catastrophising, explain WAD as sensitisation not structural damage
- Address fear of driving explicitly — graded exposure planning
- Early safe movement: graded cervical ROM, retraction exercise
- Sensorimotor retraining if vestibulo-ocular component present
- Sleep hygiene advice; pacing; safety netting

### 5. Evidence

- Icon: 🩷
- Badge: db-evid

**Key Evidence**

- **OPTIMa (Côté et al. 2017)** — Education and reassurance as core early WAD management; active approach superior to passive
- **Walton & Elliott (2017)** — WAD is heterogeneous; tailor management to individual sensitisation profile, not just QTF grade
