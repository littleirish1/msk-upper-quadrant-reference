## Scenario history

**Aoife, 38, architect.** 3-month history of ring and little finger numbness, right hand dominant. Symptoms worse during sustained desk work (drawing board) and when holding phone for long periods. Wakes at night occasionally with hand numbness. No shoulder or neck symptoms. No trauma. No history of elbow fracture. GP referred for physiotherapy.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

No visible atrophy. Sensation: reduced to light touch ring and little finger. Motor: grip and pinch grip full strength. Cubital tunnel cluster: flexion compression test positive at 2 minutes — reproduces forearm PNs. Tinel's at cubital tunnel positive. Froment's: negative. Cervical screen: negative. ULTT3 (ulnar bias): mildly sensitised right side vs left.

## Safety flags / red flags

No atrophy. No progressive motor deficit. First assessment. No concomitant health concerns.

## Legacy diagnosis

Cubital Tunnel Syndrome — Ulnar Nerve Entrapment at the Elbow

## Legacy reasoning

Ulnar border sensory symptoms provoked by sustained elbow flexion + positive cubital tunnel cluster (flexion compression + Tinel's) + no cervical involvement. No atrophy = early stage, appropriate for conservative management with close monitoring. Differentiates from CTS (median nerve, wrist provocation) and cervical radiculopathy (neck movement, dermatomal).

## Clinical traps

Treating cubital tunnel in isolation without considering the possibility of double crush or a more proximal source. C8-T1 radiculopathy can coexist with cubital tunnel syndrome — if cervical screen is positive OR if conservative management fails, cervical imaging should be considered. Also: missing the significance of work posture — the architectural drawing board is the perpetuating factor; addressing this is as important as the night splint.

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Cubital tunnel syndrome — ulnar nerve entrapment at the elbow.**

Right-handed architect presenting with ring and little finger numbness, aggravated by sustained elbow flexion at the drawing board. No motor deficit yet. Positive cubital tunnel cluster. First presentation — no concomitant health concerns.

### 2. Differential Diagnoses

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

- **Ulnar nerve entrapment at Guyon's canal** — wrist-level; distinguish by Froment's + preserved forearm sensation
- **C8-T1 radiculopathy** — neck movement involvement, dermatomal sensory pattern
- **Thoracic outlet syndrome (nTOS)** — overhead provocation, C8-T1 distribution, broader shoulder/chest symptoms
- **Medial epicondylitis** — pain dominant rather than sensory symptoms

### 3. Objective Assessment

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-obj

- Observation — hand posture, muscle wasting (hypothenar / first dorsal interosseous)
- Sensation: ring and little finger
- Motor: grip, pinch, intrinsics
- **Cubital tunnel cluster:** Flexion compression test (3 min sustained), Tinel's at cubital tunnel, Froment's sign
- Wartenberg's sign — little finger abduction at rest
- Cervical screen + neurodynamic ULTT3 (ulnar bias)
- Plain film X-ray if bony pathology suspected

**✦ 90% Note**

Atrophy is a late sign — its presence signals more advanced nerve injury and lowers the threshold for onward specialist referral urgently.

### 4. Red Flags / Safety

- Icon: 🔴
- Badge: db-safety

**Escalate If**

- Progressive motor deficit (intrinsic weakness, reduced grip)
- Atrophy (hypothenar, first dorsal interosseous)
- Rapid progression of symptoms
- Bilateral symptoms suggesting systemic or central cause

Safety netting: advise patient which symptoms require urgent reassessment. What does it mean and how long it will last are key patient questions — acknowledge uncertainty and monitor.

### 5. Management

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-mgmt

- Activity modification — avoid prolonged elbow flexion, leaning on elbow
- Night splinting — elbow extended or in neutral to offload nerve
- Padding / ergonomic advice (workstation, phone use)
- Exercise and neural mobilisation — used cautiously in acute phase
- Monitor with serial grip/pinch assessment and sensory testing
- NCS/EMG if diagnosis uncertain or progressive
- Onward referral if failing conservative management, progressive motor deficit, or atrophy
- Surgical: decompression — if conservative management fails

### 6. Communication

- Icon: 🟣
- Badge: db-comm

**Example language**

"The nerve that supplies sensation to your ring and little finger passes around the inside of your elbow. When you keep your elbow bent for long periods — at the drawing board, on your phone, sleeping — this creates pressure on the nerve. We can often manage this well with some adjustments to how you work and a splint at night. But I do want to monitor this closely — if you notice your grip weakening or wasting developing in your hand, we need to act faster."

### 7. Evidence Base

- Icon: 🩷
- Badge: db-evid

Conservative management is first-line for mild-to-moderate cubital tunnel syndrome without motor deficit. NCS/EMG should be considered when diagnosis is uncertain or to grade severity. Surgical decompression is effective for moderate-to-severe cases or failed conservative management.
