## Scenario history

**Angela, 52, administrative worker, well-controlled type 2 diabetes.** 8-week right ring finger clicking and catching when bending — sometimes locks in flexion, requires manual correction. Pain 5/10 when locks. Morning stiffness 20 minutes. No other joint involvement. GP asking about injection appropriateness.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Firm tender nodule at A1 pulley of ring finger (MCP joint palmar aspect). Active flexion: triggering reproduced — catches at 60° flexion, requires passive correction to extend. Grade III. Grip: full but avoids full flexion. No warmth or synovitis elsewhere.

## Safety flags / red flags

Type 2 diabetes — relevant for injection response and recurrence rate. Grade III triggering (locking). No systemic inflammatory features.

## Legacy diagnosis

Trigger Finger (Stenosing Tenosynovitis at A1 Pulley) — Grade III, Diabetic

## Legacy reasoning

Tender nodule at A1 pulley + active triggering reproduced + Grade III (locking requiring passive correction) = stenosing tenosynovitis. Diabetes: risk factor for trigger finger AND associated with lower resolution rates after CSI and higher recurrence. Outstanding answer integrates the diabetic context into the management decision explicitly.

## Clinical traps

Not acknowledging the diabetes context. BSSH 2016: 70% resolution with CSI — but this rate is LOWER in diabetic patients. Also: not counselling Angela that injection may temporarily raise blood glucose (relevant if on insulin or sulfonylureas). Outstanding answer includes this detail.

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Trigger finger (stenosing tenosynovitis at A1 pulley).**

Thickening of the mouth of the tendon nodule leads to roughness of the tendon surface; catches in tunnel mouth causing clicking or locking. The tendon catches as it passes through the A1 pulley. Most commonly affects ring finger > middle > thumb > others. Risk: diabetes mellitus (possible), age >40.

### 2. Key Differentials

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

- **Dupuytren's contracture:** Fixed flexion without active triggering; palmar cord palpable. Discriminator: no triggering/locking sensation; fixed deformity.
- **Flexor tendon partial tear:** Traumatic mechanism, pain with resisted flexion. Discriminator: acute onset post-trauma.
- **Rheumatoid tenosynovitis:** Bilateral, systemic RA features. Discriminator: systemic signs, multiple joint involvement.

### 3. Tests + Interpretive Value

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-obj

- **A1 pulley palpation:** Firm nodule palpable at A1 pulley (MCP joint palmar aspect). _Positive = palpable nodule, tenderness, may reproduce clicking sensation._
- **Active triggering provocation:** Ask patient to fully flex and extend affected finger. _Observe locking, catching, or incomplete extension — grades severity._

### 4. Management + Injection Decision

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-mgmt

- Splinting (night — prevent triggering position): may reduce catching for mild cases
- Corticosteroid injection: 70% resolution rate (BSSH 2016) — first-line active treatment in most cases
- Evidence: injection into tendon sheath at A1 pulley level; USS guidance improves accuracy particularly in thumb
- Surgical release: if failed 2 injections or locked/fixed trigger — A1 pulley release (excellent outcomes)
- Diabetes: higher recurrence rate after injection; may need earlier surgical referral

### 5. Evidence

- Icon: 🩷
- Badge: db-evid

BSSH 2016: corticosteroid injection 70% resolution. Surgical A1 pulley release has excellent long-term outcomes. Diabetes is associated with higher recurrence and may require earlier surgical consideration. Outstanding answer applies stepped approach: splint → injection → surgery, with diabetes as modifier for the decision timeline.
