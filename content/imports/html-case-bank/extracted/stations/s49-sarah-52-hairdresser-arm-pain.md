## Scenario history

**Sarah, 52, right-handed hairdresser.** Four-week history of right-sided neck pain radiating into the middle finger. Arm pain described as gnawing and aching, NRS 7/10 — often worse than the neck pain itself. Feeling weak when holding a hairdryer. Pain increases when working with arms above shoulder height. Cervical extension and right side flexion increase neck and arm pain. Conservative care from GP (oral NSAIDs and advice) has provided minimal benefit.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Cervical AROM: extension and right side flexion restricted and painful, reproducing arm pain. Neurological: reduced sensation middle finger right; triceps strength 4/5 right; elbow extension slightly reduced. Triceps reflex: reduced right. Spurling's test: positive right — concordant middle finger pain. ULTT: positive right. Distraction: partial relief.

## Safety flags / red flags

No bilateral symptoms. No gait change. No bowel/bladder change. No UMN signs. Progressive neurological deficit must be monitored at each review.

## Legacy diagnosis

Right C7 Cervical Radiculopathy — Occupational Context

## Legacy reasoning

Middle finger distribution = C7 dermatome. Triceps weakness = C7 myotome. Reduced triceps reflex = C7. Cervical extension + right side flexion reproduces arm pain = foraminal closure. Overhead hairdressing work = sustained cervical extension + load on C7 root. This is the official OSCE practice case (Sarah from Day 4 materials) — exact format for 27 March 2026.

## Clinical traps

Confusing C6 and C7: C6 = thumb/index/lateral forearm, biceps reflex, elbow flexion weakness. C7 = middle finger, triceps reflex, triceps/wrist flexion weakness. Sarah's middle finger distribution = C7, not C6. Also: not addressing the occupational context — hairdressing overhead work is the perpetuating factor; addressing this is as important as the exercise programme.

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Right C7 cervical radiculopathy — occupational context.**

Sarah, 52, hairdresser. Pain radiating to middle finger in a C7 dermatomal distribution. Weakness holding a hairdryer = wrist extension/elbow extension = C7 myotome. Aggravation with cervical extension and ipsilateral side flexion to the right = foraminal closure mechanism. Overhead work with arms above shoulder height = sustained cervical extension + load.

### 2. Key Differentials + Discriminator

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

- **C6 radiculopathy:** Thumb/index distribution, biceps reflex reduced, elbow flexion weakness. Discriminator: middle finger distribution = C7, not C6.
- **Median nerve entrapment:** Wrist-level provocation, nocturnal, Durkan's/Phalen's positive. Discriminator: cervical extension reproduces arm symptoms, no wrist-level provocation.
- **RCRSP:** Localised shoulder pain, painful arc, no dermatomal arm radiation. Discriminator: dermatomal middle finger radiation, cervical provocation.

### 3. Signs of Progression — Critical for OSCE

- Icon: 🔴
- Badge: db-safety

**Signs That Would Change Management Urgency**

- Progressive neurological deficit — worsening triceps weakness, increasing sensory loss
- Bilateral symptoms developing
- Upper motor neurone signs emerging (Hoffmann's, Babinski) — suggests cord involvement, escalate urgently
- Severe unremitting pain not eased by any position
- Systemic red flags: cancer history, fever, unexplained weight loss

### 4. Two Tests — Wainner Cluster for C7

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-obj

- **Spurling's test:** Cervical extension + right lateral flexion + axial compression. _Positive = concordant middle finger/arm pain reproduction = nerve root involvement. Sn ~30%, Sp ~93% — part of cluster, not standalone diagnostic._
- **ULTT median/radial bias:** _Test neural mechanosensitivity in C7 territory. Positive = concordant symptoms in middle finger/wrist extension distribution._
- **Full neurological screen:** C7 — triceps reflex, triceps strength (elbow extension), wrist flexion, middle finger sensation.

### 5. Management for Sarah — Occupational Context

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-mgmt

- Education: nerve root irritation, natural history (most improve with conservative management), occupational modifications
- Occupational advice: avoid sustained cervical extension during overhead hairdressing work; arm positioning strategies; workstation modification where possible
- Exercise: DNF control, scapular and upper quarter rehabilitation, graded upper limb loading, neurodynamic exercise (monitored for 24-hr response)
- Activity modification: pacing through working day, rest breaks, arm support
- Manual therapy as adjunct if it facilitates movement
- Clear criteria for escalation: monitoring neurology at each review

**Patient Communication + Occupational Context**

"The pain going down your arm to your middle finger is coming from a nerve being compressed where it exits your neck. The positions you hold at work — particularly reaching overhead — are putting pressure on this nerve. The good news is this usually improves with the right exercises and some adjustments to how you work. I'll give you some specific strategies for your hairdressing work. Please come back if you notice the weakness gets worse or the symptoms go into both arms."
