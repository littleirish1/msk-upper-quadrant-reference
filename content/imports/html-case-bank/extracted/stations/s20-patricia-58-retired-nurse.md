## Scenario history

**Patricia, 58, retired nurse.** 6-month difficulty with fine motor tasks — buttons, typing, handling cutlery. Legs feel heavy on stairs. Intermittent arm numbness "all over" — not clearly one distribution. No significant neck pain. Denies falls. Slight bladder urgency onset 2 months ago — attributed to UTIs. GP has diagnosed bilateral CTS. Referred to you for pre-operative physiotherapy assessment prior to bilateral carpal tunnel release.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Gait: mildly slow. Tandem gait: marginally unsteady. Upper limbs: bilateral reduced dexterity (rapid alternating hand movements difficult). Reflexes: biceps brisk bilaterally, knee jerks brisk, ankle jerks 3+. Hoffmann's sign: positive bilaterally. Babinski: equivocal right. Phalen's: negative. Durkan's: negative bilaterally.

## Safety flags / red flags

UMN signs (hyperreflexia, positive Hoffmann's, equivocal Babinski). Bilateral progressive dexterity loss. Bladder urgency. Gait change. NOT consistent with CTS alone.

## Legacy diagnosis

Probable Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (DCM) — Halt planned CTS surgery

## Legacy reasoning

Bilateral hand symptoms + leg heaviness + bladder urgency + gait change + positive UMN signs = cord involvement until proven otherwise. CTS does NOT explain UMN signs, leg symptoms, or gait changes. Proceeding to CTS surgery without excluding DCM risks harm. Urgent MRI cervical spine required. Communicate findings urgently to GP and referring surgeon. This is not a treatment session — it is a red flag recognition session.

## Clinical traps

The trap IS the scenario — proceeding with pre-operative CTS assessment without completing a myelopathy screen. The outstanding insight is that CTS cannot explain lower limb symptoms, UMN signs, or bladder urgency. A good clinician performs the operation the referral letter asks for. An outstanding clinician recognises when the presenting complaint is a red herring and acts on the full clinical picture — potentially preventing wrong surgery.

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (DCM) — early/mild presentation.**

A 58-year-old retired nurse presenting with 6 months of difficulty with fine motor tasks — struggling to do buttons and type. Reports her legs feel heavy on stairs. Intermittent non-dermatomal arm numbness. Denies significant neck pain. Her GP has been monitoring her for suspected bilateral carpal tunnel syndrome — but CTS release has been scheduled and you are her pre-operative physio assessment.

### 2. Differential Diagnoses

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

- **Bilateral carpal tunnel syndrome** — the current working diagnosis; explains hand symptoms but NOT leg heaviness or balance change
- **Peripheral neuropathy** — bilateral, but LMN pattern
- **Multiple sclerosis** — age/gender compatible; consider if disseminated symptoms
- **Motor neurone disease** — progressive; mixed UMN/LMN
- **Parkinson's disease** — gait, balance — consider if other features

### 3. Objective Assessment

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-obj

**This presentation demands a myelopathy screen before any other intervention.**

- Gait observation — tandem gait
- Upper AND lower limb neurological exam
- **UMN screen:** Hoffmann's (most sensitive — Jiang 2022), Babinski, clonus, Tromner's, Lhermitte's, finger drift
- Romberg test
- Hand dexterity observation (rapid alternating movements)
- mJOA functional grading

**✦ Critical Decision Point**

If UMN signs present: do NOT proceed with pre-operative assessment as planned. Stop. Explain your concern. Urgent referral for MRI and specialist review. Surgery for CTS should not proceed until myelopathy is assessed and excluded/confirmed.

### 4. Red Flags / Safety

- Icon: 🔴
- Badge: db-safety

**⚠ This Could Prevent Wrong Surgery**

- Bilateral hand symptoms + leg symptoms + balance change = cord involvement until excluded
- CTS does NOT explain lower limb symptoms
- Proceeding to CTS surgery without excluding DCM risks harm — myelopathy can progress rapidly post-surgery
- Urgently communicate concern to referring surgeon and GP
- Document thoroughly

### 5. Management

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-mgmt

- Halt planned surgical pathway pending DCM assessment
- Arrange urgent MRI cervical spine
- Refer via ICATS NI pathway or equivalent local pathway
- Advise patient about fall risk, avoiding cervical manipulation
- Safety net for acute deterioration (bladder/bowel, rapid weakness)
- If MRI confirms DCM: neurosurgical referral; mild myelopathy may be watched; moderate/severe = surgical consideration

### 6. Communication

### 7. Evidence Base

- Icon: 🩷

**Key Evidence**

- **Tabrah et al. 2023** — Expert consensus symptom mapping; early hand symptoms + leg involvement = red flag constellation
- **RADCeM-physio (Julia Tabrah, HRCH)** — Raising awareness of DCM among physiotherapists
- **Fehlings MG et al, Global Spine J (2017)** — AO Spine CPG: symptomatic patients with moderate/severe myelopathy benefit from surgery
- **Tetreault et al.** — Preoperative symptom duration predicts functional outcome — justifies urgency of referral

**⭐ Outstanding — The Diagnostic Trap**

The outstanding answer recognises the clinical trap: bilateral hand symptoms are easily attributed to bilateral CTS, which is common. The leg heaviness and balance change are the discriminating features — CTS cannot explain these. An outstanding practitioner holds these features in mind and acts on them even when a competing, more convenient diagnosis already exists.
