## Scenario history

**Liam, 27, office worker.** 18-month low back and morning stiffness — 90 minutes EMS. Pain improves significantly with exercise. Alternating buttock pain. Night pain in second half. Ibuprofen gives marked relief. Family history: father has psoriasis. Previously tried physiotherapy — partial improvement.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Mildly reduced lumbar lordosis. Schober's test: 2cm (normal >5cm). Bilateral SIJ tenderness. Thoracic expansion: 3cm (normal >5cm). Mild left hip restriction. No psoriasis. Neurological normal.

## Safety flags / red flags

Positive SPADE criteria: age 1hr, improves with activity, alternating SIJ pain, NSAIDs highly effective, family history psoriasis = inflammatory back pain pathway required.

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Axial Spondyloarthropathy (AxSpA) — inflammatory back pain pattern.**

The SPADE tool and inflammatory back pain pathway are the key clinical tools. Early recognition is critical — biological therapy (anti-TNF, IL-17 inhibitors) can dramatically alter disease course if started early. Physiotherapy alone misses the diagnosis and delays access to disease-modifying treatment.

### 2. Inflammatory vs Mechanical Pattern

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

- **AxSpA features:** Age 1 hour, improves with activity (not rest), alternating SIJ pain, night pain (second half), responds to NSAIDs, family history of AxSpA/psoriasis/IBD
- **Mechanical back pain:** EMS <30 minutes, worse with activity, better with rest, no systemic features
- **SPADE tool discriminators:** SpA-defining features vs non-specific low back pain — apply systematically before deciding on physiotherapy-only pathway

### 3. Why This is High Stakes

- Icon: 🔴
- Badge: db-safety

**Early Referral = Disease Modification**

Physiotherapy alone is inappropriate as the primary pathway if AxSpA is suspected. Anti-TNF and IL-17 inhibitors can halt radiographic progression if started early. A patient presenting to physiotherapy with inflammatory back pain who is managed as non-specific LBP may wait years for correct diagnosis.

**Pathway:** Apply SPADE tool → if positive: inflammatory back pain pathway → rheumatology referral → HLA-B27, MRI SIJ, CRP/ESR → specialist management ± biological therapy.

### 4. Examination

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-obj

- Posture: loss of lumbar lordosis, increased thoracic kyphosis
- Thoracic expansion: reduced (normal >5cm)
- Schober's test: lumbar flexion restriction
- SIJ palpation: bilateral SIJ tenderness
- Peripheral joint involvement: hip ROM, enthesitis screening
- Extra-articular features: eye (uveitis), skin (psoriasis), gut (IBD)

### 5. Management — Within Scope

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-mgmt

- Referral to rheumatology via SPADE/inflammatory back pain pathway
- Physiotherapy role: exercise programme (aerobic + stretching), posture management, hydrotherapy, breathing exercises, swimming
- BASFI/BASDAI outcome measures
- Education: activity is beneficial (unlike rest), swimming is particularly effective, ankylosing spondylitis support organisations
- Advise: biological therapy discussion with rheumatology when initiated
