## Scenario history

**Michael, 55, accountant.** 8-week mid-thoracic pain, 5/10 at rest. Worse after large meals and lying down at night. Partial relief with antacids. Mild postural link (overhead reach at work). No specific trauma. No neurological symptoms. Unintentional weight loss 3kg over 3 months. History of reflux and treated H. pylori 3 years ago.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Thoracic AROM: minimally restricted, pain NOT clearly modified by movement or position. Mild thoracic paraspinal tenderness T6-T8. No neurological deficit. No bony focal percussion tenderness. Mild pallor.

## Safety flags / red flags

Non-mechanical pattern (not modified by movement/position). Meal-related aggravation. Antacid partial relief. Unintentional weight loss 3kg. History of GI pathology. Multiple red flags for visceral source.

## Legacy diagnosis

Visceral Referral — Upper GI Source Masquerading as Thoracic MSK Pain

## Legacy reasoning

Meal-related + antacid relief + lying-down aggravation + prior GI history + unintentional weight loss + non-mechanical pattern = visceral source must be excluded before MSK treatment. Viscero-somatic convergence: visceral afferents converge on dorsal horn neurons shared with T6-T8 somatic afferents. Weight loss = red flag for malignancy or serious GI pathology.

## Clinical traps

Starting thoracic manual therapy because there is a partial postural link. One MSK-consistent feature does not override a cluster of visceral red flags. Outstanding answer identifies the cluster, explains viscero-somatic convergence, and does NOT treat — they refer.

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Visceral referral — gastro-oesophageal source mimicking thoracic MSK pain.**

Maintain a broad differential in thoracic presentations. If thoracic pain does NOT change with movement/position AND is associated with systemic symptoms — the visceral possibility must be actively excluded before assuming MSK cause. The clinical trap is anchoring on a partial MSK link (e.g. posture or lifting) and missing the systemic picture.

### 2. Visceral vs MSK Discriminators

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

- **Features favouring visceral:** Deep, burning, dull pain; worse after meals or at rest; not clearly modified by posture/movement; associated systemic symptoms (nausea, appetite change, weight loss, GI symptoms); constant non-mechanical pattern
- **Features favouring MSK:** Clear mechanical provocation/easing; changes with posture and movement; local palpation reproduces pain; responds to MSK assessment
- **Key differentials by zone:** Upper GI/oesophagus/stomach → mid-thoracic referral; cardiac → upper thoracic/left shoulder/jaw; biliary → right scapular referral; pancreas → band-like posterior thoracic

### 3. Do NOT Treat as MSK

- Icon: 🔴
- Badge: db-safety

**Non-Mechanical + Systemic Features = Refer Before Treating**

The outstanding answer at this station is NOT a treatment plan. It is a clear, confident explanation to the patient that this presentation needs medical review before physiotherapy, and documentation of the clinical reasoning for this decision.

**Referral pathway:** GP urgently; outline specific features that prompted concern; advise patient on symptoms that should prompt same-day emergency care (chest pain, dysphagia, haematemesis, acute worsening).

### 4. Communication

- Icon: 🟣
- Badge: db-comm

**Outstanding Communication**

"Some aspects of your pain today don't behave like a straightforward musculoskeletal problem — particularly the fact that it's related to meals and is there at rest without clear posture or movement triggers. That makes me want to make sure we're not missing something from a different part of your body before we start physiotherapy. I'm going to ask your GP to see you urgently to check this out first — that's the safest course of action."

### 5. Evidence

- Icon: 🩷
- Badge: db-evid

Thoracic pain has a higher index of suspicion for serious pathology than cervical or lumbar. Viscero-somatic convergence: visceral afferents converge on same dorsal horn neurons as somatic afferents — pain perceived as thoracic MSK. Cannot always be distinguished by movement or palpation alone. The outstanding answer applies this mechanistic understanding to justify the referral decision.
