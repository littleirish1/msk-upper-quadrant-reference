## Scenario history

**Stephen, 58, construction worker.** 4-day history of right posterior elbow swelling, 5cm, fluctuant. Gradual onset — no specific trauma recalled. Mild warmth and redness over the swelling. Pain 4/10 at rest, 7/10 with elbow flexion. No fever reported. No skin break visible. Occupation: repetitive elbow contact with hard surfaces.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Posterior elbow: 5cm fluctuant swelling over olecranon. Mild localised warmth and erythema — confined to bursa, not spreading. ROM: elbow flexion limited to 100°, extension full. No joint line tenderness. Skin intact. Temperature: 37.1°C. No systemic symptoms.

## Safety flags / red flags

Localised erythema (not spreading cellulitis). Temperature borderline. No skin break. No fever. Septic bursitis cannot be excluded clinically — aspiration for analysis may be needed.

## Legacy diagnosis

Olecranon Bursitis — Septic vs Non-Septic Differentiation Required

## Legacy reasoning

Occupational exposure + fluctuant swelling + mild localised warmth + erythema = olecranon bursitis. Localised erythema WITHOUT spreading cellulitis and WITHOUT fever = more consistent with non-septic. However — septic bursitis CANNOT be excluded clinically without aspiration. The outstanding answer does not guess — it explains the overlap and management implications of each.

## Clinical traps

Treating as definitively non-septic without considering aspiration. Localised erythema occurs in both septic and non-septic — it is not a reliable discriminator. Outstanding answer explains the clinical overlap, the consequences of missing septic bursitis (abscess, osteomyelitis), and the threshold for aspiration with antibiotic cover.

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Olecranon bursitis — septic vs non-septic differentiation required.**

The clinical distinction between septic and non-septic olecranon bursitis is a high-stakes decision. Septic bursitis requires antibiotics and possible drainage; non-septic bursitis is managed conservatively. Getting this wrong in either direction has significant consequences.

### 2. Key Differentials

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

- **Non-septic olecranon bursitis:** Trauma or repetitive pressure mechanism, fluctuant swelling, minimal systemic features, normal/mildly elevated inflammatory markers.
- **Septic olecranon bursitis:** Break in skin, significant warmth, erythema spreading beyond bursa, fever/systemic symptoms, markedly elevated CRP/WCC.
- **Gout:** Urate crystals, raised uric acid, may co-exist. Refer to rheumatology.
- **RA:** Bilateral, systemic features, rheumatoid nodules.

### 3. Septic Screen — When to Escalate

- Icon: 🔴
- Badge: db-safety

**Septic Joint Criteria — Urgent Medical Assessment**

- Significant ROM limitation — septic elbow joint likely
- Significant systemic pain
- Concomitant cellulitis spreading beyond bursa
- Fever, systemic symptoms, raised WCC/CRP
- Recent penetrating injury, IVDU, immunosuppression

Both septic and non-septic may require: aspiration for comfort; aspiration for analysis (if infective bursitis suspected — cover with antibiotics). Liaise with rheumatology if underlying gout/RA.

### 4. Assessment + Management

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-mgmt

- Non-septic: rest, ice, compression, NSAIDs, avoid direct pressure, aspiration for comfort if needed
- Septic: aspiration for analysis, antibiotics (cover Staph aureus), urgent medical assessment
- Do not aspirate in the community if septic features without medical backup
- Monitor: if non-septic fails to resolve or develops septic features → escalate

- Icon: 🩷
