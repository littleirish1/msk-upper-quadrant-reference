## Scenario history

**David, 48, IT consultant.** 14-month progressive weakness of the right hand — particularly difficulty extending right ring, little, and index fingers. No pain. Worse in cold weather — hand "doesn't work." Carpal tunnel release performed 8 months ago — no improvement. Referred for "post-operative hand rehabilitation."

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Mild right forearm extensor wasting. Right ring/little finger extension: 2/5. Index extension: 3/5. Middle/thumb extension: 4/5. Left hand: normal. Lower limb: normal. No UMN signs. Sensory: largely intact except mild ring finger dorsum reduction. Cold exposure worsens weakness visibly.

## Safety flags / red flags

Post-surgical failure. Asymmetric progressive motor deficit. Differential finger extension weakness. Cold paresis. Pure motor involvement. LMN pattern. These are NOT carpal tunnel features.

## Legacy diagnosis

Multifocal Motor Neuropathy (MMN) — Misdiagnosed as CTS, Wrong Surgery Performed

## Legacy reasoning

Progressive asymmetric distal UL weakness in specific nerve distributions + differential finger extension weakness + cold paresis + LMN findings + NO improvement after CTS release = MMN. CTS cannot explain finger extensor weakness (CTS is median/thenar motor). MMN: conduction block on neurophysiology; anti-GM1 antibodies; responsive to IVIG; WORSENS with steroids.

## Clinical traps

Commencing hand rehabilitation as a post-operative CTS case. The outstanding clinician recognises that failure to improve after CTS release combined with extensor weakness pattern and cold paresis does not fit CTS — this requires urgent neurology referral, not rehabilitation.

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Multifocal motor neuropathy (MMN) — to be recognised and referred, not treated by physiotherapy as primary pathway.**

Acquired autoimmune polyneuropathy — motor or motor predominant. Chronically progressive asymmetric weakness affecting distal upper limbs (80%). The critical clinical feature: differential finger extension weakness — stepping pattern of weakness following specific peripheral nerve distributions. Often initially misdiagnosed as MND.

### 2. Clinical Features — Pattern Recognition

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

- Chronically progressive asymmetric weakness — distal UL 80%
- Stepwise progression over months-years
- Finger extensors > flexors — this is the clinical hallmark
- Differential finger extension weakness pattern
- Worsening with cold exposure ("cold paresis") — 80% of MMN patients (Slee 2007)
- LMN findings: fasciculations and cramps common (Vlam 2012)
- NO pain phase (distinguishes from PTS)
- Anti-GM1 antibodies positive in ~50%
- Neurophysiology: conduction block — critical diagnostic finding

### 3. Misdiagnosis Danger

- Icon: 🔴
- Badge: db-safety

**Critical: MMN Worsens With Steroids**

MMN responds to IV immunoglobulin (long-term maintenance required). However, it WORSENS with corticosteroids. If a patient with undiagnosed MMN is given steroids for presumed inflammatory condition, the disease will progress. This is why correct diagnosis is clinically critical.

MMN vs ALS flail arm variant: Many MMN patients initially misdiagnosed as ALS. ALS: mixed UMN/LMN; split hand sign; no response to IVIG. MMN: pure LMN; conduction block on neurophysiology; responsive to IVIG.

### 4. Examination

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-obj

- Detailed upper limb motor examination — test each finger independently
- Look for pattern: asymmetric, specific peripheral nerve distributions, NOT dermatomal
- Differential finger extension: is weakness in specific finger extensors while others are preserved?
- Temperature-sensitive weakness: re-examine after cold exposure if possible
- LMN signs: wasting, fasciculations, absent/reduced reflexes — NO UMN signs

### 5. Management Role

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-mgmt

- Physiotherapy role is SUPPORTIVE — not primary treatment
- Urgent neurology referral for IVIG consideration
- Rehabilitation: maintain function, prevent secondary complications, adaptive strategies
- Education: avoid cold exposure, energy conservation
- DO NOT prescribe high-intensity strengthening — disease-modifying treatment should come first

### 6. Evidence

- Icon: 🩷
- Badge: db-evid

Slee et al. (Neurol 2007): differential finger extension weakness in MMN. Vlam et al. 2012: neurophysiology — conduction block is diagnostic. Key advanced practice principle: when progressive asymmetric distal upper limb weakness does NOT fit a dermatomal, myotomal, or central pattern — think MMN. Refer to neurology before any physiotherapy programme. IVIG is the treatment, not exercise.
