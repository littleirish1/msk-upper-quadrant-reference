## Scenario history

**Clinical reasoning and justification station.** Examiner asks: "You want to use cervical mobilisation as part of treatment for this patient with cervicogenic headache. Justify your decision and explain the evidence base."

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Patient has positive FRT, upper cervical hypomobility, and CGH diagnosis confirmed. You plan to use C1-C2 SNAG or upper cervical PA mobilisation as part of a combined approach with DNF exercise.

## Safety flags / red flags

Pre-manipulation vascular screen: negative. No contraindications identified.

## Legacy diagnosis

Manual Therapy Justification — Evidence-Based Reasoning

## Legacy reasoning

Manual therapy is justified as an adjunct to exercise, not standalone treatment. Mechanisms are multiple and non-specific (neurophysiological, psychological, movement facilitation). JMMT 2021 supports combined approach. Modern framing: facilitates movement and exercise confidence, not structural correction. Safety screen documented and negative — appropriate to proceed.

## Clinical traps

Citing the evidence for manual therapy without acknowledging its limitations. A good answer says 'manual therapy has evidence for CGH'. An outstanding answer cites JMMT 2021, explains the proposed mechanisms are multiple and non-specific (neurophysiological, psychological, movement facilitation), and acknowledges that combining with exercise produces better outcomes than manual therapy alone — and that manual therapy should not be used as a standalone intervention.

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Evidence-Based Justification

- Icon: 🩷
- Badge: db-evid

A 90% answer states that manual therapy is best viewed as one part of multimodal care rather than a highly specific corrective technique aimed at a presumed positional fault.

**✦ Key Principles**

- **Safety** — appropriate screen before application
- **Comfort** — shared decision-making; patient preference
- **Efficiency** — adjunct to support movement and exercise, not a standalone
- **Person-centred communication** — explain effects in accessible, non-biomechanical language

**Module Reading List**

- **Journal of Manual & Manipulative Therapy (2021)** Vol 29(6):376-389 DOI:10.1080/10669817.2021.1937813 — Contemporary manual therapy evidence base

**⭐ Outstanding — Nuanced Critical Appraisal**

Proposed mechanisms of manual therapy are multiple and non-specific: neurophysiological, psychological, placebo, movement facilitation. The evidence supports combining with exercise. Modern practice de-emphasises "fixing" structural faults — emphasises facilitating movement confidence and exercise engagement.

### 2. How to Explain to a Patient

- Icon: 🟣
- Badge: db-comm

**Accessible Language**

"I'm going to use some hands-on treatment to help your joint move more easily and reduce some of the protective tension in the muscles. Think of it as helping your body get ready to do the exercises — the exercises are the main treatment."

### 3. Key Reasoning Points

- Icon: 🟢
- Badge: db-reason

- Manual therapy is an **adjunct**, not the whole treatment plan
- Proposed mechanisms are multiple: neurophysiological, psychological, non-specific
- Best evidence supports combining with exercise rather than using alone
- Modern practice de-emphasises "fixing" structural faults and emphasises facilitating movement and confidence
