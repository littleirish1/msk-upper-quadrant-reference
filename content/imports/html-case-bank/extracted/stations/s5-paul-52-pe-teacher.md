## Scenario history

**Paul, 52, PE teacher.** 5-month right shoulder pain, insidious onset. Pain over the lateral deltoid, worse with overhead activities — specifically throwing and putting up basketball hoops. Night pain when lying on right side. No trauma. No previous dislocation.

Tried rest for 6 weeks with minimal improvement. GP prescribed NSAIDs — some benefit. Active and motivated to return to sport.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Active ROM: painful arc 70-120°, full range achievable with pain. Passive ROM: full range, mild discomfort at end-range elevation. Resisted ER: painful but full strength. Empty can: painful, 4+/5. Hawkins-Kennedy: positive. Cervical screen: negative. AC joint: non-tender, cross-body negative. No atrophy.

## Safety flags / red flags

No trauma suggesting acute full thickness tear requiring urgent imaging. No red flags. No cervical contribution.

## Legacy diagnosis

Rotator Cuff Related Shoulder Pain (RCRSP)

## Legacy reasoning

Non-traumatic, overhead-aggravated shoulder pain with painful arc and positive cuff loading tests. Passive ROM preserved (rules out capsulitis). No cervical contribution. No AC joint involvement. RCRSP is an umbrella term — Lewis 2016 emphasises test results do not reliably isolate specific structures. Exercise-based management is first-line.

## Clinical traps

Using the RCRSP label without challenging the impingement narrative. Patients often arrive with 'impingement' labelling from previous clinicians — an outstanding answer reframes this explicitly ('we now understand this as load-related tendon sensitivity rather than mechanical impingement') and uses Lewis 2016 to justify. Also: missing the cervical screen — 15% of apparent shoulder pain has a cervical contribution.

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Rotator cuff related shoulder pain (RCRSP).**

A non-traumatic shoulder pain presentation characterised by pain during elevation, overhead difficulty, night pain, and reduced function. RCRSP is an umbrella term — a 90% answer recognises it is more clinically useful than over-specific tissue labelling.

### 2. Differential Diagnoses

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

- **Adhesive capsulitis** — if marked restriction of both active and passive ROM, often in a capsular pattern
- **Glenohumeral OA** — older adults; stiffness, crepitus, reduced passive movement
- **Cervical referral** — if symptoms extend beyond shoulder with neurological or neck-related provocation

### 3. Objective Assessment

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-obj

- Observation
- Active ROM
- Passive ROM
- Symptom behaviour at range
- Resisted cuff loading
- Functional tasks
- Cervical screen

**✦ 90% Note**

Shoulder special tests do not reliably isolate single structures and should be interpreted within the whole clinical picture — not used as binary diagnostic tests.

### 4. Red Flags / Safety

- Icon: 🔴
- Badge: db-safety

**Screen For**

- Infection
- Suspected unreduced dislocation
- Tumour / malignancy
- Acute traumatic tear with major weakness
- Non-shoulder referred pain

### 5. Management

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-mgmt

**First-line:**

- Education
- Load management
- Progressive exercise therapy
- Address beliefs and expectations
- Analgesia discussion within scope

**Exercise:** Graded ROM → progressive loading → restore function → scapular and upper quarter rehabilitation where relevant

**Manual therapy:** Adjunct if it facilitates movement or exercise

### 6. Communication

- Icon: 🟣
- Badge: db-comm

Avoid false precision. Explain that RCRSP is a useful clinical umbrella guiding rehab even if one exact tissue source cannot always be identified.

**✦ 90% Level**

Challenge the idea that a label like "impingement" means something is being physically trapped. The evidence has moved on — explain this accessibly.

### 7. Evidence Base

- Icon: 🩷
- Badge: db-evid

Exercise-based management is supported for non-traumatic cuff presentations and is often at least comparable to surgery in appropriate groups. Imaging is not routinely required early unless trauma suggests a significant tear or conservative treatment fails.
