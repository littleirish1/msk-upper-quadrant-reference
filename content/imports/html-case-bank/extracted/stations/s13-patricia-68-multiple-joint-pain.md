## Scenario history

**Patricia, 68.** Generalised pain and stiffness in both hands for 3 years. Affects multiple finger joints and both thumbs. Stiffness worse in the morning (20-30 min). Functional impact: difficulty with buttons, opening jars, typing. No systemic features. No skin changes. CRP, ESR, RF: normal on bloods. X-ray: joint space narrowing at PIP and DIP joints bilaterally, osteophytes.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Heberden's nodes at DIPs. Bouchard's nodes at PIPs. Bony swelling — no warmth or synovitis. CMC grind: bilateral, positive. Grip strength: reduced bilaterally. ROM: mildly restricted at affected joints.

## Safety flags / red flags

Normal inflammatory markers + distribution at DIP/PIP/CMC = OA not inflammatory arthritis. No systemic features.

## Legacy diagnosis

Hand Osteoarthritis (Nodal OA)

## Legacy reasoning

Nodal distribution (Heberden's + Bouchard's), normal inflammatory markers, bony swelling without warmth/synovitis, imaging consistent = hand OA. Differentiate from RA (MCP involvement, synovitis, elevated CRP/RF, systemic features) and psoriatic arthritis (DIP but with psoriasis, nail changes, enthesitis). Management: exercise, joint protection, topical NSAIDs, splinting, education.

## Clinical traps

Attributing all finger joint pathology to OA without screening for inflammatory arthritis. Heberden's/Bouchard's nodes at DIP/PIP with normal inflammatory markers = OA. But elevated CRP/RF, MCP involvement, warmth/synovitis, or systemic features should redirect to inflammatory pathway. Also: haemochromatosis arthropathy specifically involves MCP 2&3 — if that's the distribution, EULAR 2025 criteria should be applied.
