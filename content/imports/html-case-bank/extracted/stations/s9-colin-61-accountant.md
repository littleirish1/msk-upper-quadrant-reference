## Scenario history

**Colin, 61, accountant.** 10-week constant mid-thoracic pain, 6/10 at rest. No specific mechanism. Worse at night — wakes him. Not clearly aggravated or eased by movement or posture. Also mentions loss of appetite and has lost 4kg in the last 2 months unintentionally. History of renal cell carcinoma — treated 5 years ago, told he was "clear."

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Thoracic AROM: minimally restricted, pain not significantly changed by movement. Thoracic palpation: focal tenderness T6-T8. No neurological deficit. No UMN signs.

## Safety flags / red flags

TUNAFISH: H (cancer history), U (unexplained weight loss 4kg), A (age >50), non-mechanical night pain. MULTIPLE RED FLAGS PRESENT.

## Legacy diagnosis

Suspected Spinal Metastasis — URGENT REFERRAL

## Legacy reasoning

Cancer history + non-mechanical night pain + unexplained weight loss = metastatic until proven otherwise. 70% of spinal metastases are thoracic. X-ray is insensitive for early metastases. MRI required urgently. This is NOT a physiotherapy treatment case — explanation and urgent referral is the correct management. Finucane et al JOSPT: red flags interpreted in context, but this cluster demands immediate action.

## Clinical traps

Treating the thoracic region as the primary complaint and starting a manual therapy assessment without screening the TUNAFISH flags. In this station the multiple red flags (cancer history, non-mechanical night pain, unexplained weight loss) mean that no MSK treatment is appropriate. The trap: focusing on the postural link and the stiffness rather than the systemic picture. A good answer identifies red flags; an outstanding answer explains that X-ray is insensitive for early metastases and that MRI is required.
