## Scenario history

**Sarah, 34, office manager.** 3-week history of neck pain and stiffness following a rear-end collision on the motorway. Car was stationary, struck at approximately 30mph. Seatbelt worn, airbag deployed. No LOC.

Neck stiffness worse in the morning. Intermittent dizziness (non-rotatory). Headache since accident. Feeling anxious about driving again. Sleep poor. Right arm feels "heavy" but no dermatomal pattern. Worried about permanent damage.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Cervical AROM: globally restricted, pain-limited. No dermatomal sensory loss. Reflexes normal and symmetric. No UMN signs. ULTT: mildly sensitised bilaterally. Neurodynamic testing: non-specific response. VBI screen: negative. Cervical palpation: global tenderness, most pronounced C2-C5 bilaterally.

## Safety flags / red flags

Dizziness requires VBI screen (done — negative). No progressive neurology. No myelopathy signs. Canadian C-Spine criteria: fracture risk assessed — low risk, no imaging required.

## Legacy diagnosis

Whiplash Associated Disorder (WAD) — Grade II

## Legacy reasoning

Mechanism consistent with WAD. Grade II: musculoskeletal signs present (restricted ROM, tenderness) but no neurological deficit. Dizziness may reflect cervicogenic or vestibulo-ocular disruption rather than vascular compromise. Psychosocial yellow flags present (anxiety, fear of driving, catastrophising). QTF Grade II; biopsychosocial management indicated.

## Clinical traps

Treating WAD as a purely biomechanical problem and missing the psychosocial yellow flags. A good answer gives a graded exercise programme. An outstanding answer recognises the fear of driving, sleep disruption, and catastrophising as clinical targets that are as important as the cervical dysfunction — and acknowledges that the dizziness needs a vascular explanation before vestibulo-ocular attribution.

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Whiplash Associated Disorder (WAD).**

Supported by the mechanism of injury and cluster of symptoms including neck stiffness, headache, dizziness, movement sensitivity, and psychosocial distress. Symptoms may reflect pain, sensorimotor disruption, vestibulo-ocular disturbance, and fear-related responses.

### 2. Differential Diagnoses

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

- **Cervical fracture/instability** — must be screened early depending on trauma severity
- **Concussion / mild TBI** — consider if cognitive, visual, vestibular or headache symptoms are prominent
- **Cervical vascular pathology** — must be excluded if vascular features or risk profile raise suspicion

### 3. Objective Assessment

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-obj

Assessment should be **tailored to irritability**.

- Cervical ROM
- Neurological screen
- Balance testing
- Smooth pursuit / oculomotor assessment if indicated
- Cranial nerve screen where relevant
- Movement behaviour and functional observation

### 4. Red Flags / Safety

- Icon: 🔴
- Badge: db-safety

**Screen For**

- Fracture risk (Canadian C-Spine / NEXUS criteria)
- Vascular features
- Cranial nerve changes
- Progressive neurology
- Concussion-type presentation

### 5. Management

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-mgmt

**Early priorities:**

- Education and reassurance
- Reduce fear and perceived damage
- Encourage early safe movement
- Graded return to activity
- Address sleep and coping

**Rehabilitation:**

- Graded cervical exercise
- Sensorimotor retraining if indicated
- Balance and oculomotor rehabilitation where appropriate
- Pacing and functional progression

### 6. Communication

- Icon: 🟣
- Badge: db-comm

Validate the patient's symptoms while avoiding over-medicalisation. A strong answer acknowledges pain and distress while supporting active recovery.

**✦ 90% Level**

Recognise WAD as biopsychosocial — not a pure tissue damage problem. Address fear of movement and catastrophising explicitly.

### 7. Evidence Base

- Icon: 🩷
- Badge: db-evid

**Module Reading List**

- **Côté P, Wong JJ et al (OPTIMa), Eur Spine J (2017)** DOI:10.1007/s00586-016-4467-7 — CPG: management of neck pain and associated disorders. Education and reassurance as core early management

- **Quebec Task Force (1995/2019)** — WAD classification grades I-IV

**⭐ Outstanding — Critical Appraisal**

Cite the QTF classification but acknowledge Walton & Elliott's critique — WAD is heterogeneous and cannot be managed as a single entity. Tailor to the individual's pain sensitisation profile, psychosocial factors, and irritability level.
