## Scenario history

**Ryan, 22, rugby player.** Injured thumb in a match yesterday — tackled and fell on outstretched hand, thumb forced into abduction. Immediate pain and swelling at the MCP joint of the thumb. Unable to grip or pinch properly. Coach applied ice. First presentation today.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Swelling and bruising over the ulnar aspect of the thumb MCP joint. Tenderness: ulnar collateral ligament and MCP joint. X-ray: no fracture. Valgus stress test at 30° flexion: increased laxity with soft end-feel compared to contralateral side — suggests complete rupture. Pinch grip: significantly weakened.

## Safety flags / red flags

Possible complete UCL rupture — Stener lesion must be considered. Imaging referral required.

## Legacy diagnosis

Acute Thumb UCL Injury — possible complete rupture (Gamekeeper's/Skier's Thumb)

## Legacy reasoning

Forced abduction mechanism + ulnar MCP tenderness + increased valgus laxity with soft end-feel = UCL injury. Soft end-feel suggests complete rupture — Stener lesion possible (adductor pollicis aponeurosis interposes, preventing healing). BEST Guidelines 2023: MRI or USS to confirm degree of injury and guide surgical vs conservative decision. Immobilisation pending specialist review.

## Clinical traps

Stress testing a potentially completely ruptured UCL — a Stener lesion means the adductor pollicis aponeurosis interposes, preventing healing; surgical repair timing matters. A good answer diagnoses UCL injury; an outstanding answer identifies the need to determine complete vs partial rupture (soft end-feel = complete), recognises Stener lesion risk, and refers appropriately rather than immobilising and waiting.
