## Scenario history

**Various headache/cervical presentations may be tested.** Scenario A: 45yo with new onset severe headache "like a thunderclap," worst headache of his life, onset during exertion. Scenario B: 38yo pre-manipulation cervical screen — reports occasional dizziness with head movement.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Scenario A: BP 178/104. No neck stiffness on this visit but patient very distressed. Scenario B: VBI screen — dizziness reproduced on sustained left rotation. Nystagmus noted. No other CN findings.

## Safety flags / red flags

Scenario A: thunderclap headache = SAH until proven otherwise — emergency. Scenario B: positive VBI screen = contraindication to cervical manipulation.

## Legacy diagnosis

A: Subarachnoid Haemorrhage (Emergency) / B: Positive Cervical Vascular Screen

## Legacy reasoning

Thunderclap headache + exertion onset + hypertension = SAH. 999/emergency department immediately. Do not proceed with any MSK assessment. Scenario B: IFOMPT Cervical Framework (Rushton, Kerry et al JOSPT 2023) — positive VBI screen = modify approach, no manipulation, consider urgent medical review.

## Clinical traps

Scenario A: failing to identify thunderclap + exertion onset as SAH until proven otherwise. The 'worst headache of my life' onset description is classic — but clinicians can be led astray by an apparently calm patient or prior migraine history. Outstanding answer: stop assessment, 999 immediately, do not proceed with any further MSK evaluation. Scenario B: continuing with manipulation after a positive VBI screen because 'the symptoms are probably cervicogenic'. A positive screen = contraindication — period.

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Distinction-Level Screen

- Icon: 🔴
- Badge: db-safety

- **SNOOP / SNOOP10** awareness
- 3Ds, 3Ns and ataxia / vascular considerations
- BP if indicated
- Full neuro screen
- Cranial nerve assessment where symptoms justify it

**Why Cranial Nerve Testing Matters**

Cranial nerve dysfunction may indicate neurological or vascular pathology beyond routine MSK headache. CN III palsy (ptosis, pupil dilation) → posterior communicating artery aneurysm. CN XII deviation → medullary lesion.

### 2. Vascular Screening

- Icon: 🟢
- Badge: db-reason

Before cervical manipulation, IFOMPT framework should be applied. Pre-manipulation vascular screen includes:

- 3Ds: dizziness, diplopia, dysarthria
- 3Ns: nausea, numbness, nystagmus
- Ataxia
- Dysphagia

A positive screen = contraindication to manipulation. Document and modify approach.
