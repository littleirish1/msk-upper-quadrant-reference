## Scenario history

**Patricia, 68, retired, long-standing RA.** Currently on methotrexate and hydroxychloroquine. 6-week new onset neck pain and occipital headaches, worse with head movement. New dizziness when turning head. Referred by rheumatologist for physiotherapy neck pain management.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Gentle cervical AROM only (clinical suspicion high — full assessment NOT completed). Mild restriction all planes. Upper limb neurology: normal. Lower limb: bilateral brisk reflexes. Hand dexterity: slightly reduced. Gait: mildly unsteady. No plain film imaging available.

## Safety flags / red flags

RA + new cervical symptoms + dizziness with head movement + bilateral brisk lower limb reflexes + gait unsteadiness = possible atlantoaxial instability or cervical myelopathy. Imaging MUST be obtained before cervical physiotherapy proceeds.

## Legacy diagnosis

Suspected Craniocervical Instability in RA — Imaging Required Before Physiotherapy

## Legacy reasoning

RA is the most common cause of acquired craniocervical instability. Inflammatory erosion of transverse ligament → risk of atlantoaxial subluxation → cord compression. ADI >3mm = abnormal; >6-8mm = significant instability risk. Dizziness + brisk reflexes + gait unsteadiness = possible cord involvement. Cervical physiotherapy WITHOUT imaging is unsafe.

Proceeding with cervical manual therapy or end-range cervical assessment because the rheumatologist sent a routine referral. Outstanding clinician does not proceed — explains clinical reasoning, arranges imaging, modifies approach. ## Legacy domain content

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Craniocervical instability in a patient with rheumatoid arthritis.**

RA is the most common cause of acquired craniocervical instability in adults. Inflammatory erosion of the transverse/alar/apical ligaments of the atlantoaxial complex leads to subluxation risk. This is one of the most dangerous presentations in MSK physiotherapy — standard cervical assessment and manual therapy are CONTRAINDICATED until stability is confirmed radiologically.

### 2. Why RA Creates This Risk

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

- RA targets synovial joints and ligamentous attachments — atlantoaxial joint is a synovial joint
- Transverse ligament erosion → anterior atlantoaxial subluxation (most common)
- ADI (atlantodental interval) >3mm in adults = abnormal; >6-8mm = transverse ligament disruption risk
- Vertical subluxation: cranial settling — basilar invagination
- Patient may be unaware of this risk — RA spine involvement often asymptomatic initially

### 3. URGENT — Do Not Proceed Without Imaging

- Icon: 🔴
- Badge: db-safety

**⚠ Cervical Assessment Requires Prior Imaging in RA**

- Do NOT perform cervical AROM to end range, PPIVMT, cervical manipulation, or traction in a patient with RA without first reviewing recent cervical spine X-rays (flexion/extension views + open mouth odontoid)
- Sharp-Purser test: use of this test is NOT supported — lacks reliability, validity, and safety
- If imaging not available: perform only a brief, gentle, non-end range neurological screen; arrange urgent imaging before proceeding

**Symptoms of atlantoaxial instability:** Feeling like they "need to hold their head on," occipital headache, dizziness, myelopathic symptoms, neck pain with head movement.

### 4. Examination — Modified Approach

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-obj

- Neurological screen: upper and lower limb, UMN tests — this takes priority
- Observe posture and head position carefully
- DO NOT apply overpressure or end-range cervical loading until imaging reviewed
- Check for signs of cord involvement: clumsiness, gait change, bladder symptoms
- Review medications: long-term methotrexate, biologics — immunosuppression risk

### 5. Management

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-mgmt

- Await imaging before cervical physiotherapy programme
- If instability confirmed: liaison with rheumatology and spinal surgery
- If stable on imaging: gentle, controlled cervical exercise — no high-velocity techniques
- Broader RA management: SARAH programme (strengthening and stretching for RA of the hand — NICE recommended 2021)
- Advise patient: always inform any treating clinician of RA diagnosis before cervical treatment

### 6. Evidence

- Icon: 🩷
- Badge: db-evid

Craniocervical instability in RA: prevalence of atlantoaxial subluxation in RA populations is significant. The Sharp-Purser test: bottom line — use NOT supported (Haunschild et al. systematic review: lacks reliability, validity, and safety). Imaging (XR flexion/extension, open mouth odontoid views; MRI for soft tissue; CT for bony detail) is the only safe way to proceed. Outstanding answer cites the Sharp-Purser evidence and explains why clinical testing cannot replace imaging in this population.
