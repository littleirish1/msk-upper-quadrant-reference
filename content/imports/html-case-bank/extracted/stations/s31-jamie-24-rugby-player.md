## Scenario history

**Jamie, 24, rugby player.** Direct fall onto right shoulder during a tackle 5 days ago — landed on the point of the right shoulder. Immediate pain over the top of the shoulder. Visible step deformity at AC joint. Pain 6/10 at rest, 9/10 with any arm elevation. Unable to carry kit bag. Wants to return to contact rugby.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Step deformity AC joint right. Focal AC joint tenderness. Active abduction painful 40-60°. Passive ROM full but painful. Scarf test: positive. O'Briens + Paxons: positive. Horizontal stability: mild instability present. No neurovascular deficit. X-ray: widened AC joint space.

## Safety flags / red flags

No neurovascular compromise. Grade III suspected from horizontal instability. Orthopaedic opinion needed for contact sport return.

## Legacy diagnosis

Acromioclavicular Joint Injury — Rockwood Grade III

## Legacy reasoning

Direct contact mechanism + visible step deformity + horizontal instability + widened AC joint on X-ray + CC ligament suspected disruption = Grade III. Horizontal instability (ISAKOS type 3B) raises surgical discussion in contact athlete. O'Briens + Paxons in series: Sn 95.8%, Sp 82% (Krill 2019).

## Clinical traps

Treating Grade III as Grade I-II without considering the surgical decision in a contact athlete. Key discriminator: horizontal instability indicates CC ligament involvement. Good answer manages conservatively; outstanding answer identifies the surgical decision point and refers appropriately.

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Acromioclavicular joint injury — Rockwood Grade II-III.**

Direct contact mechanism with injury to AC ligament complex. Grade II: AC ligament tear, CC ligaments intact, mild step deformity. Grade III: both AC and CC ligaments torn, visible step deformity. Horizontal (A-P) instability evaluates AC ligaments; vertical (S-I) instability evaluates CC ligaments. Key decision: does this need surgical review?

### 2. Key Differentials + Discriminator

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

- **Clavicle fracture:** Midshaft tenderness, X-ray confirms. Discriminator: palpation localised to AC joint not clavicular shaft.
- **RCRSP:** Gradual onset, no contact mechanism, AC joint non-tender. Discriminator: mechanism and focal AC joint tenderness.
- **Shoulder dislocation:** GHJ involvement, apprehension, loss of contour. Discriminator: AC joint tenderness with step deformity — humeral head still seated.

### 3. Safety &amp; Referral Decision

- Icon: 🔴
- Badge: db-safety

**Rockwood Grading Drives Referral**

- Grade I-II: conservative management appropriate
- Grade III: shared decision-making — conservative management is often first-line; surgical referral if contact athlete or significant functional deficit at 6-8 weeks
- Grade IV-VI: urgent orthopaedic referral
- Horizontal instability (ISAKOS type 3B): indicates need for more aggressive treatment

### 4. Two Tests + Interpretive Value

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-obj

- **Scarf test:** Cross-body adduction. _Positive = AC joint pain reproduction. Sn 77%, Sp 79% (Huijbregts 2013)._
- **O'Briens test + Paxons in series:** _Sn 95.8%, Sp 82% (Krill 2019) for ACJ pathology when performed in series._
- **Horizontal and vertical stability assessment:** Differentiates Grade II (CC intact) from Grade III (CC torn) — critical for surgical decision.

### 5. Management + Return to Contact Sport

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-mgmt

- Phase 1 (weeks 1-2): Pain management, sling comfort, gentle pendulum
- Phase 2 (weeks 2-4): Active ROM, scapular stabilisation, anterior deltoid and trapezius activation
- Phase 3 (weeks 4-6+): Progressive loading, sport-specific conditioning, contact preparation
- Return to contact: pain ≤2/10 with full ROM, strength ≥90% contralateral, sport-specific testing
- Protective equipment (AC donut/pad) for return to contact sport

### 6. Evidence

- Icon: 🩷
- Badge: db-evid

Farrell (2020): ACJ injuries 10% of all shoulder injuries, 40-50% in athletic population. Highest incidence 20-30yr. ISAKOS type 3B horizontal instability indicates need for more aggressive treatment consideration (Krill 2019 — O'Briens + Paxons: Sn 95.8%, Sp 82%).
