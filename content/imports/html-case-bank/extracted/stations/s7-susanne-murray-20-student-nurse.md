## Scenario history

**Susanne Murray, 20, student nurse and horse rider.** Right shoulder "giving way" since a fall from her horse 8 months ago. Reports 4 further subluxation episodes since, often when reaching overhead or with arm in abduction and ER. Apprehension prevents her from riding. Also mentions her other joints are "loose" — recurrent ankle sprains, hypermobile fingers. Diagnosed with hEDS at 17. Under financial and academic pressure.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Beighton Score: 6/9. Apprehension test: strongly positive in abduction + ER — apprehension (not just pain). Relocation: reduces apprehension. Sulcus sign: 1.5cm bilaterally. Load and shift: grade II anterior laxity right shoulder. Skin: hyperextensible, soft, mild atrophic scars on knees. No axillary nerve deficit.

## Safety flags / red flags

No acute neurovascular compromise. No unreduced dislocation. Assess axillary nerve post any acute episode.

## Legacy diagnosis

Traumatic Anterior Shoulder Instability on background of hEDS (Stanmore Type II)

## Legacy reasoning

Recurrent instability following trauma + apprehension in ABER position = anterior instability. Background hEDS = Stanmore Type II (atraumatic structural component). Beighton 6/9 confirms generalised hypermobility. Management differs from Type I (purely traumatic) — surgical outcomes less predictable in EDS; neuromuscular rehabilitation is primary focus.

## Clinical traps

Treating EDS instability the same as traumatic instability without recognising the Stanmore Type II classification difference. The key trap: recommending the same surgical pathway (Bankart/Latarjet) as you would for Type I trauma. In Type II (EDS), surgical outcomes are less predictable, genetics input is essential, and neuromuscular rehabilitation is the primary focus (Jaggi & Lambert 2010). Also: not addressing the psychosocial context (academic pressure, financial impact, horse riding goals).

### 3. Objective Assessment (Susanne Murray Case)

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-obj

- **Beighton Hypermobility Score** (9-point) — quantifies generalised hypermobility; changes rehab approach in suspected EDS
- **Apprehension + Relocation** — APPREHENSION (not just pain) = positive → anterior instability
- **Sulcus Sign** — inferior laxity; bilateral = MDI
- **Scapular kinematics / Load and Shift** — dynamic control deficit
- **Observation** — skin quality, posture, muscle wasting, winging

**✦ EDS Modification**

In suspected EDS — standard tests may underestimate laxity extent. Beighton is essential. Multiple joint assessment. Connective tissue features (skin hyperextensibility, scarring, easy bruising).

### 5. Management (Early / Mid / End Stage)

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-mgmt

**Early:** Pain management, education on instability mechanism, submaximal RC isometrics, scapular setting. Avoid provocative positions (overhead, ER in abduction).

**Mid:** Progressive RC loading, scapular stabilisers (serratus anterior, lower/mid trapezius), proprioception, functional movement patterns.

**End:** Sport/task-specific loading, dynamic stability under perturbation, return-to-activity criteria, self-management.

**Barriers (EDS case):** Slower collagen healing; higher laxity baseline; depression + exam pressure; MVP limits intensity; financial impact of work absence.

**If not responding:** MRI arthrogram (labral pathology); liaison with orthopaedics re Bankart/Latarjet; genetics input before surgical decision in EDS.

### 6. Communication

- Icon: 🟣
- Badge: db-comm

Explain recurrence risk, the need for rehabilitation, and how decisions are influenced by age, activity level, recurrence history, and goals.

In the 20F EDS case — acknowledge the academic pressure, financial impact, and horse riding goals explicitly.

**⭐ Outstanding — EDS Nuance**

Stanmore Type II (EDS) — surgical outcomes less predictable than in the general population. Genetics input is essential before surgical decision-making. This is the nuanced point that distinguishes outstanding from good answers on this station.

### 8. Evidence Base

- Icon: 🩷

Surgical outcomes (Bankart repair / Latarjet) in EDS are less predictable than in the general population — genetics input is essential before surgical decision. For traumatic instability without EDS, recurrence rates are high with conservative management in young active individuals — shared decision-making re surgery is appropriate after adequate rehab trial.
