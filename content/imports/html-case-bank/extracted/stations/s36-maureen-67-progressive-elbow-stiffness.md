## Scenario history

**Maureen, 67, retired receptionist.** 3-year progressive bilateral elbow stiffness. Right worse than left. Cannot fully extend right elbow. Intermittent locking sensation right elbow. Pain 3-4/10 with activities requiring full extension. Previous right elbow fracture age 45 — "was pinned." Minimal morning stiffness (10 minutes). No systemic features.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Right elbow: flexion 130°, extension deficit -25°. Passive ROM mirrors active. Firm capsular end-feel. Mild crepitus. Mild joint line tenderness. No warmth or synovitis. Grip: full. Neurology: normal.

## Safety flags / red flags

Extension deficit with firm end-feel + previous fracture = post-traumatic OA likely. Intermittent locking = loose body possible — X-ray indicated.

## Legacy diagnosis

Post-Traumatic Elbow Osteoarthritis

## Legacy reasoning

Previous ORIF age 45 + progressive stiffness + firm capsular end-feel + extension deficit + crepitus + bilateral but worse on injured side = post-traumatic OA. Previous fracture/dislocation hastens elbow OA onset. Intermittent locking = possible loose body — requires X-ray.

## Clinical traps

Managing as simple stiffness without imaging the intermittent locking — loose body requires arthroscopic washout if confirmed. Also: not applying NICE OA principles — but locking IS an imaging indication here, distinguishing from routine OA where imaging is not routinely indicated.

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Elbow osteoarthritis — primary or post-traumatic.**

Limitation of elbow flexion and/or extension, stiffness, excessive capsular fibrosis, possible bone spur formation. Previous fracture or dislocation can hasten onset. Typical conservative approach — arthroscopy or joint replacement relatively uncommon compared to hip/knee.

### 2. Key Differentials

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

- **Inflammatory arthritis:** Bilateral, systemic features, warmth/synovitis — check RF, CRP. Discriminator: RA can affect elbow.
- **Loose body:** Intermittent locking, clicking — X-ray may show calcification or osteochondral fragment.
- **Olecranon bursitis:** Posterior swelling over olecranon, ROM often relatively preserved.

### 3. Assessment + Management

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-mgmt

- AROM, PROM — characterise the pattern of restriction and painful arc
- Functional assessment: occupational demands, specific limitations
- Education: natural history of elbow OA, realistic expectations
- Exercise: maintain and improve ROM, strengthening within available range
- Activity modification, ergonomic advice, assistive devices
- Referral: BESS surgical guidelines for primary and revision elbow replacement (2023) — generally conservative first; arthroscopy/replacement reserved for significant functional limitation

### 4. Evidence

- Icon: 🩷
- Badge: db-evid

Elbow OA: conservative management is first-line. Arthroscopy/joint replacement not common. BESS DePuy Surgical Guidelines (2023) for primary and revision elbow replacement. Outstanding answer applies OA management principles (NICE NG226): activity-related diagnosis, conservative management priority, avoid over-investigation.
