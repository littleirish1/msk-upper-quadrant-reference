## Scenario history

**Lisa, 46.** Nocturnal numbness and tingling in thumb, index, and middle fingers, waking nightly. Shaking hand provides relief. Daytime symptoms with driving and keyboard work. Reduced pinch strength and dropping objects. Wants rapid symptom control and discussion of injection and onward referral criteria.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Observation: no visible thenar atrophy. Durkan's compression test: positive — median nerve symptoms reproduced within 15 seconds. Phalen's test: positive at 30 seconds. APB strength: 4/5 right vs 5/5 left (mildly reduced). Thenar bulk: subjectively mildly reduced right vs left. Cervical screen: negative. No dermatomal sensory loss above wrist.

## Safety flags / red flags

Mild thenar motor involvement (reduced APB strength, possible mild atrophy). This is high irritability CTS with motor component — monitor closely and set clear escalation criteria.

## Legacy diagnosis

Carpal Tunnel Syndrome (CTS) — High Irritability with Thenar Motor Involvement

## Legacy reasoning

Classic nocturnal median distribution symptoms + Phalen's/Durkan's positive + wrist-level aggravation (driving, keyboard) = CTS. Reduced APB strength and possible mild thenar reduction = thenar motor involvement. No cervical involvement. The motor component elevates urgency — irreversible thenar atrophy is a threshold for urgent surgical referral.

## Clinical traps

Giving the injection without explaining the evidence framework. The outstanding answer does not simply say yes or no to the injection request — it explains: CSI gives short-term relief but no long-term benefit (NICE CKS), Lewis 2020 shows therapist-led pathway reduced conversion to surgery from 80% to 59%, and in the presence of motor involvement the surgical referral conversation should happen now rather than waiting for failure. Not monitoring thenar status closely — this is the escalation trigger.

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Carpal Tunnel Syndrome (CTS) — high irritability with possible thenar motor involvement.**

Three features: (1) nocturnal thumb/index/middle finger symptoms waking nightly + relief with shaking = classic CTS pattern; (2) daytime symptoms with sustained flexed wrist posture (driving, keyboard) = wrist position-dependent median nerve compression; (3) reduced pinch strength and dropping objects = possible thenar motor involvement — raises urgency.

### 2. Key Differentials + Discriminator

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

- **C6 radiculopathy:** Neck movement provocation, dermatomal above wrist, reduced biceps reflex. _Against here:_ purely distal wrist-level aggravation, no neck symptoms, classic nocturnal CTS pattern.
- **Systemic cause (bilateral):** Hypothyroid, RA, amyloidosis. _Screen if bilateral._ Single-sided here — but ask about contralateral symptoms.

### 3. Safety — Thenar Atrophy Threshold

- Icon: 🔴
- Badge: db-safety

**Motor Involvement — Escalation Criteria**

- Examine thenar bulk carefully — compare bilateral
- Test APB strength specifically (median nerve thenar branch)
- Thenar atrophy = irreversible motor loss → urgent surgical referral
- Safety net: if weakness progresses rapidly or atrophy develops → escalate immediately

### 4. Two Tests + Interpretive Value

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-obj

- **Durkan's compression test:** Direct firm pressure over carpal tunnel up to 30 seconds. _Positive = median nerve symptom reproduction in thumb/index/middle = CTS. More specific than Tinel's at wrist._
- **Phalen's test:** Sustained wrist flexion 60 seconds. _Positive = symptom reproduction in median distribution. Note: false positives possible with prolonged pressure — interpret cautiously._

### 5. Management + Injection &amp; Referral Discussion

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-mgmt

- Neutral wrist night splint — first-line; immediate symptom control
- Activity modification: ergonomic advice for driving and keyboard
- Nerve and tendon gliding exercises
- CSI discussion: short-term relief confirmed, no long-term benefit; appropriate bridge to surgery for high irritability with motor involvement
- Lewis et al. (2020): therapist-led pathway reduced conversion to surgery (59% vs 80% at 24 weeks)
- Onward referral criteria: thenar atrophy, failed 6-8 weeks conservative, progressive motor deficit

### 6. Evidence &amp; Communication

- Icon: 🩷
- Badge: db-evid

**Key Evidence**

- **Lewis LK et al. (2020)** — Therapist-led pathway: 59% surgery vs 80% control at 24 weeks
- **NICE CKS** — Night splint first-line; CSI short-term; surgical referral for motor involvement

**Communication — Injection Discussion**

"The most important thing first is a night splint — this removes the pressure at night and often makes a significant difference quickly. We can also discuss an injection, which gives fast relief, but research shows it doesn't change the long-term outcome the way structured treatment does. We'll keep a close eye on the strength in your thumb — if that gets worse, we'd want to get you seen by a surgeon sooner rather than later."
