## Legacy domain content

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Cervicogenic headache.**

Supported by temporal relationship between neck symptoms and headache; aggravation with cervical posture or loading; reduced cervical ROM; and probable upper cervical involvement. Less suggestive of migraine where photophobia, phonophobia, nausea or aura are not dominant.

### 2. Differential Diagnoses

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

- **Migraine** — if episodic, severe, with sensory sensitivity, nausea or aura
- **Tension-type headache** — often bilateral, pressing, less clearly linked to neck movement
- **Secondary headache from serious pathology** — must always be screened

### 3. Objective Assessment

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-obj

- Cervical ROM
- **Flexion Rotation Test (FRT)** — restriction + symptom reproduction toward ipsilateral side = C1-C2 dysfunction
- Upper cervical palpation / symptom provocation
- Deep neck flexor endurance
- Neurological screen
- Cranial nerve screen if indicated

### 4. Red Flags / Safety

- Icon: 🔴
- Badge: db-safety

**Screen For (SNOOP/SNOOP10)**

- Sudden onset headache ("thunderclap")
- New or changing headache pattern
- Neurological deficits
- Vascular symptoms
- Cranial nerve abnormality
- Systemic features
- Abnormal BP where indicated

### 5. Management

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-mgmt

- Education — cervical contribution to headache
- Exercise targeting cervical mobility and endurance
- Manual therapy as adjunct if appropriate
- Posture and activity strategies
- Self-management plan

### 6. Communication

- Icon: 🟣
- Badge: db-comm

Explain that the headache appears linked to the neck, and that treatment focuses on improving function rather than simply chasing pain.

**Example language**

"The headache you're experiencing appears to be coming from the upper part of your neck. The good news is that this type of headache usually responds well to physiotherapy that targets the neck. We'll also look at your posture and movement patterns."

### 7. Evidence Base

- Icon: 🩷
- Badge: db-evid

The physiotherapy role is strongest in cervicogenic headache. High-level answers combine red flag screening with active rehabilitation and education. FRT has good sensitivity/specificity for CGH versus other primary headache types when used within a diagnostic cluster.

**Module Reading List**

- **Jull G et al, Cephalalgia (2007)** 27:793-802 — Cervical musculoskeletal impairment in frequent intermittent headache. Validates cervical contribution to CGH
- **IHS ICHD-3 (2021)** — International Classification of Headache Disorders. Diagnostic gold standard for CGH vs migraine vs tension-type vs cluster
- **BASH (2019)** — National Headache Management System for Adults. UK clinical guideline
- **Silberstein SD, Cephalalgia (2004)** 24(Suppl.2):2-7 — Migraine pathophysiology. Underpins mechanistic differentiation from CGH
- **Dodick D, Rozen T, Goadsby P, Silberstein S, Cephalalgia (2000)** 20(9):787-803 — Cluster headache diagnostic features
- **Haider S et al, Clinical Medicine (2024)** DOI:10.1016/j.clinme.2023.100005 — Novel secondary headache screening tool for acute care
- **Migraine Trust (2024)** — Research on impact of migraine; under-recognition despite prevalence
