## Scenario history

**Eleanor, 68, retired teacher.** Severe right shoulder OA with intact rotator cuff. Listed for reverse geometry shoulder replacement in 6 weeks. Current ROM: flexion 65°, abduction 50°, ER 10°. Significant night pain. Reports anxiety about operation. Smokes 10/day. BMI 28. Referred for prehabilitation.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Global ROM restriction in capsular pattern. Deltoid strength 4/5. Grip preserved. Neurological normal. Pre-operative anxiety evident. Oxford Shoulder Score: 18/48.

## Safety flags / red flags

Smoking cessation required — increased anaesthetic and wound healing risk. Moderate pre-operative anxiety identified. These are modifiable prehabilitation targets.

## Legacy diagnosis

Pre-Operative Physiotherapy — ERAS Prehabilitation Prior to Reverse Geometry Shoulder Replacement

## Legacy reasoning

ERAS framework: Villers & Burch 2020 — prehabilitation improves shoulder co-ordination/strength, prepares psychologically, optimises physical health, improves self-efficacy. Rauk & Rautarsky 2020: low mental/physical well-being pre-operatively negatively affects perceived post-op outcome. Eleanor's smoking and anxiety are both modifiable targets that outstanding answer addresses explicitly.

## Clinical traps

Focusing entirely on physical exercise and missing the psychological preparation component. Low self-efficacy and pre-operative anxiety strongly predict poorer post-operative outcomes. Also: not preparing Eleanor for the specific post-operative precautions — after reverse arthroplasty, avoid extension, internal rotation, adduction. The operation note and surgeon's preference drives rehabilitation, not a calendar protocol.

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Clinical Context

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Pre-operative physiotherapy (prehabilitation) prior to reverse geometry shoulder replacement.**

ERAS (Enhanced Recovery After Surgery) framework applies. Prehabilitation improves post-operative outcomes — Villers & Burch 2020. RAPSODI trial: comparing reverse vs anatomical replacement for painful shoulder OA with intact RC.

### 2. Prehabilitation Goals

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-obj

- Improve shoulder co-ordination and control
- Maximise shoulder strength — deltoid and any intact RC
- Prepare patient psychologically for post-operative experience
- Optimise physical health: cardiovascular fitness, nutrition, smoking cessation
- Improve self-efficacy — Rauk & Rautarsky 2020: low mental/physical well-being pre-operatively negatively affects perceived post-op outcome
- Establish baseline outcome measures: Oxford Shoulder Score, VAS, ROM

### 3. Early Post-Operative Rehabilitation (0-6 weeks)

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-mgmt

- Goal: osseous integration of implant, allow tissues to heal
- Check operation note and surgeon's preference — CRITICAL
- Identify safe zone — stipulated by surgeon (typically: no extension, no internal rotation, no adduction)
- Scapular exercises, elbow/wrist/hand movements within safe zone
- Sling use per surgeon protocol
- Progressive ROM introduction based on healing stages — not calendar-driven

### 4. Communication — Boardering for Surgery

- Icon: 🟣
- Badge: db-comm

**Shared Decision-Making Conversation**

"Before your operation, there are some things we can do together to give you the best possible chance of a good recovery. Research shows that patients who prepare physically and mentally beforehand tend to have better outcomes. No two people are the same — so our plan will be tailored to what you can do and what matters most to you. There are no guarantees with surgery, but we can optimise your starting point."

### 5. Evidence

- Icon: 🩷
- Badge: db-evid

**ERAS principles:** Pre-operative optimisation, healthy habits, prehabilitation exercises, nutritional guidance. **RAPSODI Trial** — reverse vs anatomical replacement for painful shoulder OA: multi-centre pragmatic superiority RCT (430 patients, 24 months, England/Wales/NI). **Villers & Burch 2020** — prehabilitation prior to shoulder surgery improves outcomes.
