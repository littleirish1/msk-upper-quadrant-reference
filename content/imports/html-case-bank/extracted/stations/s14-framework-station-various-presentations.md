## Legacy diagnosis

## Legacy reasoning

Outstanding answers don't just list red flags — they explain which cluster in THIS patient raises concern, what the prior probability of serious pathology is, and what the referral pathway is. Subtle flags matter: night pain that is non-positional, bilateral symptoms developing from a unilateral onset, neurological signs that are progressing.

## Clinical traps

Listing red flags as isolated items rather than explaining how they interact in THIS patient's clinical profile. An outstanding answer uses the Finucane et al. (JOSPT) framework — contextual interpretation, not tick-box screening. The distinction: a single red flag in isolation may have low prior probability; a cluster of flags in a specific patient profile shifts the probability dramatically.

### 2. How Red Flags Should Be Used

- Icon: 🟢
- Badge: db-reason

Not as isolated checkboxes. They must be interpreted in context, using:

- Symptom cluster
- Risk profile
- Severity and progression
- Response to care
- Level of clinical concern

**✦ 90% Level**

Clinical reasoning drives the decision to treat, monitor, or escalate. A single red flag in isolation may not be sufficient — the clinical gestalt matters. But one high-risk flag (myelopathy signs, cancer history + night pain, neurological deterioration) should trigger urgent action.
