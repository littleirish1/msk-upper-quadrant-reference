## Scenario history

**Kevin, 48, carpenter.** Six-week history of right neck pain radiating to lateral forearm, thumb and index finger. Paraesthesia in thumb and index finger. Reports weakness using a screwdriver and reduced grip endurance.

Symptoms worse with overhead work, looking up, and turning head to the right. Night waking 2–3 times. Eased by supporting arm on head and by gentle neck distraction. No gait change, no bowel or bladder change, no bilateral symptoms.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Cervical AROM: extension and right rotation limited and painful, reproducing arm symptoms. Neurological exam: reduced sensation lateral forearm and thumb; elbow flexion 4/5 right; wrist extension 4/5 right; biceps reflex reduced right side. Spurling's test: positive right — concordant arm pain reproduction. Cervical distraction: symptom relief. ULTT median bias: positive right — concordant symptoms at 40° elbow extension vs 70° left. No UMN signs. No bilateral symptoms.

## Safety flags / red flags

No bilateral symptoms. No gait change. No bowel/bladder change. No UMN signs. No systemic features.

## Legacy diagnosis

Right C6 Cervical Radiculopathy

## Legacy reasoning

C6 root level confirmed by: (1) dermatomal distribution to thumb/index = C6 dermatome; (2) weakness of wrist extension + elbow flexion = C6 myotome; (3) reduced biceps reflex = C6 reflex. Positive Wainner cluster (Spurling + ULTT + distraction relief + rotation restriction). Bakody sign (arm on head relief) = foraminal decompression. No UMN signs — cord not involved.

## Clinical traps

Not completing the neurological examination to confirm C6 root level specifically. Naming the condition without explaining why it fits THIS patient's presentation. Focusing only on treatment without addressing the red flag screen (myelopathy must be actively excluded, not just assumed absent). A good answer gives the diagnosis — an outstanding answer explains the specific features that confirm C6, and why this cannot be DCM.

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Right C6 radiculopathy.**

Three securing features: (1) dermatomal symptoms to thumb and index finger = C6 dermatome; (2) weakness with screwdriver use (wrist extension + supination) = C6 myotome; (3) aggravation with extension/ipsilateral rotation, relief with shoulder abduction (Bakody sign) = foraminal decompression.

### 2. Key Differentials + Discriminator

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

- **RCRSP / Shoulder referred pain:** Localised to shoulder, painful arc, normal neurological exam, not dermatomal. _Against here:_ clear dermatomal radiation, cervical provocation reproduces arm symptoms.
- **CTS / Median nerve entrapment:** Nocturnal hand symptoms, wrist-level provocation, Durkan's/Phalen's positive, no neck provocation. _Against here:_ arm pain from neck, Bakody sign, cervical extension reproduces symptoms.
- **C6 radicular pain without deficit:** Acceptable — arm pain in C6 distribution without objective neurological deficit. Distinction from radiculopathy affects prognosis framing.

### 3. Safety, Red Flags &amp; Progression

- Icon: 🔴
- Badge: db-safety

**Screen For**

- Myelopathy features: gait change, hand clumsiness, UMN signs — confirm absent
- Progressive neurological deficit — quantify and monitor each visit
- Severe unremitting pain not responding to any position change
- Systemic red flags: fever, weight loss, cancer history

**Signs of progression requiring escalation:** Worsening weakness, new bilateral symptoms, gait change, bladder/bowel involvement, positive UMN signs on any review → urgent specialist referral.

### 4. Two Objective Tests + Interpretive Value

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-obj

Choose two from Wainner cervical radiculopathy cluster — must interpret for this specific patient:

- **Spurling's test:** Extension + ipsilateral lateral flexion + axial compression. _Positive = concordant arm symptom reproduction = nerve root involvement. High specificity (~93%), low sensitivity (~30%) — useful for ruling in, not ruling out._
- **ULTT median bias:** Shoulder depression → abduction → ER → wrist/finger extension + cervical contralateral flexion. _Positive = concordant thumb/index symptoms = neural mechanosensitivity in C6/median distribution._
- **Cervical distraction:** Manual traction. _Relief of arm symptoms = foraminal decompression supports nerve root involvement._
- **Full neurological exam:** Biceps reflex (C6), elbow flexion and wrist extension myotome, lateral forearm/thumb sensation. Compare sides.

### 5. Management Plan

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-mgmt

- Education on prognosis — most C6 radiculopathy resolves with conservative management; explain nerve root irritation clearly
- Activity modification and pacing — avoid sustained cervical extension/rotation, overhead work with arms elevated
- Directional preference and symptom-modifying positions — arm abduction/support if helpful
- Exercise: deep neck flexor control, graded upper limb loading as tolerated, scapular/upper quarter rehabilitation, neurodynamic exercise (monitor 24-hr response)
- Manual therapy as adjunct to facilitate movement — not standalone
- Clear progression/regression criteria based on 24-hour response

### 6. Evidence &amp; Communication

- Icon: 🩷
- Badge: db-evid

**Key Evidence**

- **NICE CKS (2023)** — Cervical radicular pain: conservative management, imaging thresholds, escalation criteria
- **Kjær P et al. (2017) Eur Spine J** — National guidelines for non-surgical treatment of neck pain and cervical radiculopathy
- **Wainner RS et al. (2003) Spine, 28(1)** — Diagnostic cluster: 3+ positive tests = high PPV for cervical radiculopathy

**Plain Language Communication**

"The pain going down your arm is coming from a nerve being irritated where it exits your neck — this is what's causing the numbness in your thumb and fingers and the weakness you've noticed. The good news is most people with this improve with the right exercises and guidance. Contact us urgently if weakness gets worse, symptoms appear in both arms, or you notice any changes to your walking or bladder."
