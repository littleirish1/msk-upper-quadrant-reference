## Scenario history

**Patrick, 72, retired mechanic.** 8-year right shoulder pain. 3 months ago: sudden severe worsening after lifting a heavy engine part. Now unable to raise arm above waist. Pain 5/10 at rest, 8/10 with any attempt at elevation. Previous GP assessment: "just wear and tear."

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Mild right shoulder atrophy. Active abduction 30° only, forward flexion 40°. Passive ROM: full achievable with discomfort. ER lag sign: positive right. Drop arm test: positive. Belly press: equivocal. Neurology: normal. X-ray: subacromial narrowing, no fracture.

## Safety flags / red flags

No red flags. Traumatic worsening on background of degenerative disease. MRI needed to assess tear extent and Goutallier fatty infiltration grade before any surgical decision.

## Legacy diagnosis

Massive Rotator Cuff Tear — likely Goutallier grade 3-4 fatty infiltration

## Legacy reasoning

8-year background + acute worsening + positive ER lag sign (Sn 94% — Miller 2008) + positive drop arm test + age 72 + male + heavy occupation = massive cuff tear with high probability of significant fatty infiltration. Goutallier grade 3-4 means primary repair has poor prognosis — this changes the surgical decision fundamentally.

## Clinical traps

Referring directly for surgery without acknowledging that Goutallier grade drives the surgical decision. Grade 3-4 fatty infiltration means primary repair fails at high rates — the outstanding answer knows this and frames the management conversation accordingly. Also: Hinsley 2022 — 48.4% of full-thickness tears are asymptomatic; Frost 1999 — cannot identify symptomatic patients from MRI alone.

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Massive rotator cuff tear with likely Goutallier grade 3-4 fatty infiltration.**

Age 72, traumatic mechanism, profound weakness in abduction and ER, positive ER lag sign and belly press — pattern consistent with large/massive supraspinatus + infraspinatus involvement. MRI fatty infiltration data changes the surgical decision-making fundamentally.

### 2. Key Differentials + Discriminator

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

- **RCRSP without tear:** Painful but strength relatively preserved, negative lag signs. Discriminator: ER lag sign Sn 94%, Sp 46% (Miller 2008) — positive here.
- **Adhesive capsulitis:** Equal passive restriction in capsular pattern. Discriminator: passive ROM full here — RCRSP/tear.
- **Neuralgic amyotrophy:** Sudden severe pain phase then weakness, scapular winging. Discriminator: traumatic mechanism, no scapular winging, no acute neuropathic pain phase.

### 3. Surgical Decision — Imaging Drives Management

- Icon: 🔴
- Badge: db-safety

**Goutallier Grade 3-4 Changes Everything**

Fatty infiltration >50% = irreversible degeneration. Primary repair has poor prognosis. Surgical options narrow significantly. This is NOT a straightforward 'refer for surgery' decision.

- Goutallier grade 3-4: high re-tear rates post-repair; limited surgical viability for primary repair
- Options: reverse shoulder arthroplasty if conservative management fails; superior capsule reconstruction in select cases
- Evidence: Hinsley 2022 — 48.4% of full-thickness tears asymptomatic. Frost 1999 — cannot identify symptomatic patients from MRI alone

### 4. Key Tests

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-obj

- **ER lag sign:** _Passive ER to end range → patient holds → positive = lag = infraspinatus/teres minor rupture. Sn 94%, Sp 46% (Miller 2008)._
- **Belly press test:** _Hand pressed into abdomen — inability to maintain = subscapularis tear. Sn 34%, Sp 96% (Kappe 2018)._
- **Drop arm test:** _Passive 90° abduction → patient lowers slowly — unable to control = significant supraspinatus disruption._

### 5. Management

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-mgmt

- Active rehabilitation is first-line even in the presence of massive tear — JOSPT 2025 guidelines
- Patient education: options, realistic expectations, evidence on conservative vs surgical outcomes
- Exercise: motor control, compensatory muscle activation (subscapularis, teres minor, deltoid), scapular stability
- Function-focused: what does this patient need to do — 72yr retired mechanic vs 35yr manual worker drives different goals
- Liaison with orthopaedics for surgical discussion — reverse arthroplasty if conservative management fails
- Outcome measure: Oxford Shoulder Score, DASH, PROMS

### 6. Evidence

- Icon: 🩷
- Badge: db-evid

**Key Evidence**

- **Paavola 2021, Beard 2018 (CSAW)** — ASD no better than sham; subcromial decompression evidence base undermined
- **Hinsley 2022** — 48.4% full-thickness tears asymptomatic; severity unrelated to pathology unless >2.5cm
- **JOSPT 2025** — Active rehabilitation first-line for RCRSP; as effective as surgery/injections long-term
