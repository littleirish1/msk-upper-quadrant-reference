## Scenario history

**Dorothy, 74, on long-term prednisolone for RA.** Sudden onset severe central thoracic pain while lifting a bag of compost in the garden — heard a "crack." Pain 8/10, worse with any movement, lying down, coughing. Cannot find a comfortable position. Previous DEXA: T-score -2.8 (osteoporosis).

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Observation only (high irritability): marked thoracic kyphosis. Percussion: focal bony tenderness T8. Minimal movement assessment — all movements severely limited and painful. No neurological deficit on brief screen. Vital signs stable.

## Safety flags / red flags

TUNAFISH: T (trauma — minor), S (steroid use), A (age >50). Mechanism + osteoporosis risk = fracture until proven otherwise.

## Legacy diagnosis

Thoracic Osteoporotic Vertebral Compression Fracture

## Legacy reasoning

Sudden onset on minor exertion, known osteoporosis, long-term steroid use, age 74, bony percussion tenderness = VCF until proven otherwise. X-ray first line; MRI if cord involvement suspected or pathological fracture needs excluding. Do NOT over-examine — modify approach based on suspected diagnosis. Bone protection referral and fracture risk management essential.

## Clinical traps

Performing a full active movement assessment in an acute suspected VCF. The examination approach must match the clinical concern — over-assessing an acute osteoporotic fracture risks causing harm. An outstanding answer explicitly modifies the assessment approach based on the clinical hypothesis and justifies why imaging must precede any rehabilitation.
