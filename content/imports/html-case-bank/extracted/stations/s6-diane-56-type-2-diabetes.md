## Scenario history

**Diane, 56, with Type 2 diabetes.** 8-month progressive left shoulder stiffness. Initially very painful; pain now somewhat reduced but stiffness is the dominant problem. Unable to reach behind her back or across her body. Difficulty with dressing.

Insidious onset — no trauma. Sleep disturbed. Tried physio elsewhere (deep tissue massage) with no improvement. Frustrated and asking "will this ever get better?"

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Active ROM: flexion 95°, abduction 80°, ER 10°, IR limited to L4. Passive ROM mirrors active — firm capsular end-feel. Pattern: ER most restricted, then abduction, then IR. Resisted testing: full strength throughout. Cervical screen: negative.

## Safety flags / red flags

No red flags. Capsular pattern with diabetes = adhesive capsulitis until proven otherwise.

## Legacy diagnosis

Adhesive Capsulitis (Frozen Shoulder) — Frozen Phase

## Legacy reasoning

Classic capsular pattern of restriction (ER > ABD > IR) with firm end-feel. Diabetes is a significant risk factor — associated with more severe and prolonged course. In the frozen phase: pain reduced, stiffness dominant. Distinguishes from RCRSP (passive ROM preserved in RCRSP) and GHJ OA (crepitus, older, imaging).

## Clinical traps

Missing the metabolic/systemic link. Frozen shoulder in a diabetic patient has a worse prognosis, longer duration, and different collagen biology — an outstanding answer addresses this explicitly and adjusts prognosis framing accordingly. Also: not staging the patient — treatment in the frozen phase (stiffness dominant) differs from the painful/freezing phase (irritability dominant).

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Adhesive capsulitis / frozen shoulder.**

Gradual onset pain followed by progressive stiffness, particularly affecting external rotation and limiting both active and passive movement. Metabolic associations such as diabetes strengthen suspicion.

### 2. Differential Diagnoses

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

- RCRSP
- Glenohumeral OA
- Post-traumatic stiffness
- Serious pathology if atypical

### 3. Objective Assessment

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-obj

- Active ROM
- Passive ROM
- Compare irritability and end feel
- Assess pattern of restriction (ER > ABD > IR = capsular pattern)
- Cervical screen

**✦ 90% Note**

Stage and irritability matter. Overly aggressive stretching in the painful/freezing stage may worsen symptoms and reduce compliance.

### 4. Management

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-mgmt

- Education on natural history and staging
- Symptom management (analgesia, sleep positioning)
- Graded ROM work according to irritability
- Consider injection pathway depending on stage, local governance, scope
- Focus on function and pacing

### 5. Communication

- Icon: 🟣
- Badge: db-comm

Patients must understand that frozen shoulder is often prolonged and staged, and that recovery usually takes time. Avoid over-promising timelines.

**Example language**

"Frozen shoulder tends to go through stages — a painful phase, a stiff phase, and then a thawing phase. The good news is that most people do recover, but it can take 12-18 months. Our aim is to manage symptoms and keep your shoulder as functional as possible through this process."
