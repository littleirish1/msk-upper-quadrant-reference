## Scenario history

**Anne, 62, retired teacher.** Six months of increasing hand numbness in both hands, dropping objects, difficulty with buttons, and reduced handwriting control. Reports unsteady gait on uneven ground and difficulty on stairs. Neck pain is mild and intermittent. Reports new urinary urgency over the last month. No recent trauma. Symptoms are not dermatomal.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Gait: unsteady, wide-based. Tandem gait: unable to complete safely. Upper limbs: bilateral reduced dexterity, difficulty with rapid alternating movements. Reflexes: brisk biceps, triceps, knee jerks bilaterally. Hoffmann's: positive bilaterally. Babinski: equivocal bilaterally. Clonus: sustained at bilateral ankles. No clear dermatomal sensory loss.

## Safety flags / red flags

Multiple UMN signs bilaterally. Bilateral progressive neurological deficit. Gait disturbance. Bladder urgency. ALL are red flags. This is not a routine MSK physiotherapy case.

## Legacy diagnosis

Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (DCM)

## Legacy reasoning

Bilateral hand dexterity loss + lower limb gait disturbance + bladder urgency + UMN signs (Hoffmann's, hyperreflexia, clonus) = cord dysfunction until proven otherwise. Non-dermatomal pattern distinguishes from bilateral radiculopathy. CTS cannot explain lower limb or bladder symptoms. The correct response today is urgent referral, not conservative management.

## Clinical traps

The most dangerous trap: attributing the bilateral hand symptoms to bilateral CTS because that is the common explanation. CTS cannot explain gait disturbance, clonus, or bladder urgency. If you treat this as bilateral CTS, you delay DCM diagnosis and risk progressive irreversible cord damage. The outstanding clinician holds ALL the features simultaneously and recognises that no peripheral cause explains this combination.

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Degenerative Cervical Myelopathy (DCM).**

Three securing features: (1) bilateral hand symptoms with dexterity loss = bilateral motor dysfunction at cord level; (2) gait disturbance and difficulty on stairs = lower limb cord involvement; (3) new urinary urgency = sphincter dysfunction. Non-dermatomal pattern distinguishes from radiculopathy.

### 2. Key Differentials + Discriminator

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

- **Bilateral CTS:** Median distribution, wrist provocation, no leg involvement. _Against here:_ bilateral non-dermatomal + gait change + bladder urgency cannot be explained by CTS.
- **Peripheral polyneuropathy:** Length-dependent, symmetrical, LMN signs. _Against here:_ UMN signs expected; gait pattern is upper motor neurone.
- **MS:** Younger onset typically, disseminated lesions on MRI. _Against here:_ age 62, degenerative history — MRI will differentiate.

### 3. Immediate Safety Action

- Icon: 🔴
- Badge: db-safety

**⚠ Stop Routine MSK Pathway**

Urgent onward referral for medical assessment, specialist review, and urgent MRI cervical spine. This is not a routine physiotherapy treatment case. Clear safety netting and documentation essential.

**UMN signs to confirm:** Hoffmann's (most sensitive), Babinski, clonus, hyperreflexia throughout, tandem gait, Romberg, rapid alternating hand movements.

### 4. Three Objective Signs — Rationale

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-obj

- **Hoffmann's sign:** Flick middle finger → watch thumb/index flexion. Positive = corticospinal tract involvement = UMN lesion. Most sensitive UMN test (Jiang et al. 2022).
- **Tandem gait / Romberg:** Dorsal column → proprioceptive deficit → gait ataxia → cord involvement at cervical level.
- **Rapid alternating hand movements:** Difficulty = dexterity impairment from cord-level dysfunction. Tests the symptom the patient is presenting with.

### 5. Communication Task

- Icon: 🟣
- Badge: db-comm

**Outstanding Communication — Today's Appointment**

"I need to share something important with you today. Some of what you've described — the difficulties with your hands, the unsteadiness on stairs, and the bladder urgency — suggests this may be more than a routine neck problem. It's possible there is some pressure on your spinal cord rather than just a nerve. I think it's really important that you have an urgent specialist assessment and a scan. This is so we can understand exactly what's happening and make sure we're treating this in the right way."

Check understanding. Manage anxiety. Advise: avoid falls risk, avoid cervical manipulation, seek urgent care if deterioration. Document findings and conversation thoroughly.

### 6. Evidence Base

- Icon: 🩷
- Badge: db-evid

**Rationale for Urgent Referral**

- **Fehlings MG et al. (2017) Global Spine J** — CPG for DCM: surgery for moderate/severe myelopathy prevents irreversible cord damage
- **Tabrah J et al. (2023) RADCeM-physio** — Expert consensus: >70% wait ≥6 months for diagnosis; delay leads to poorer outcomes
- **Tetreault et al.** — Longer preoperative symptom duration = worse post-surgical functional outcome — directly justifies urgency
