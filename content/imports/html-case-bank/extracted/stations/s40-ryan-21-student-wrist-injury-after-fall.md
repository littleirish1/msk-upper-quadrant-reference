## Scenario history

**Ryan, 21, student.** Fell onto outstretched right hand playing football yesterday. Immediate radial wrist pain and swelling around the base of the thumb. Pain 6/10 at rest, 8/10 with gripping. Attended A&E — plain film reported "no fracture identified." Discharged as "wrist sprain." Referred to physiotherapy today.

## Scenario examination / objective findings

Mild swelling radial side. Exquisite tenderness in anatomical snuff box (ASB). Scaphoid compression test: positive. AROM limited by pain. Neurological normal. No thenar wasting.

## Safety flags / red flags

CRITICAL: Negative plain film X-ray does NOT exclude scaphoid fracture in first 10-14 days. ASB tenderness post-FOOSH = treat as fracture until MRI proves otherwise. AVN risk if missed.

## Legacy diagnosis

Suspected Scaphoid Fracture — Negative X-Ray Does Not Exclude

## Legacy reasoning

Young patient + FOOSH + exquisite ASB tenderness + positive scaphoid compression test = scaphoid fracture until proven otherwise by MRI. Plain film insensitive in first 2 weeks — a negative X-ray does NOT exclude fracture. This patient was appropriately X-rayed but incorrectly discharged as wrist sprain without imaging follow-up.

## Clinical traps

Accepting the A&E "no fracture" diagnosis without applying the clinical principle: ASB tenderness post-FOOSH = scaphoid fracture until MRI proves otherwise. Outstanding answer actively challenges the previous diagnosis on clinical grounds.

## Legacy domain content

### 1. Most Likely Diagnosis

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

**Suspected scaphoid fracture — treat as fracture until proven otherwise.**

Scaphoid = most commonly fractured carpal bone. FOOSH mechanism. Anatomical snuff box tenderness is the characteristic clinical feature. The critical clinical principle: plain film X-ray is insensitive in the first 2 weeks. A negative X-ray does NOT exclude scaphoid fracture. Clinical suspicion alone warrants management as fracture pending definitive imaging.

### 2. Key Differentials

- Icon: 🔵
- Badge: db-diag

- **Radial styloid fracture:** Tenderness more distal, X-ray confirms. Discriminator: location of maximum tenderness.
- **TFCC injury:** Ulnar-sided, different mechanism. Discriminator: radial-sided ASB tenderness here.
- **De Quervain's:** No acute mechanism, APL/EPB tenderness. Discriminator: acute FOOSH, bony ASB tenderness.

### 3. AVN Risk — Why This is High Stakes

- Icon: 🔴
- Badge: db-safety

**Retrograde Blood Supply = AVN Risk**

Blood enters scaphoid distally via retrograde flow. Fracture through waist can disrupt proximal blood supply. Proximal pole AVN = potential avascular necrosis — potentially career-ending for young active patients if missed.

**Management if suspected:** Thumb spica splint immediately. Urgent secondary care referral. If plain film negative but clinical suspicion high: MRI (most sensitive, confirms fracture within 24-48 hours) or CT (bony detail). Do NOT discharge without clear safety net.

### 4. Clinical Tests

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-obj

- **Anatomical snuff box palpation:** _Positive = tenderness in ASB (triangular depression at base of thumb where scaphoid articulates with radius). High sensitivity for scaphoid fracture._
- **Scaphoid compression test:** Axial compression along thumb ray. _Positive = pain at scaphoid level._
- **Plain film X-ray:** _May be negative in first 10-14 days — a negative X-ray does NOT exclude fracture. Clinical suspicion drives management._

### 5. Management

- Icon: 🟡
- Badge: db-mgmt

- Immediate: thumb spica splint, urgent secondary care referral
- Definitive imaging: MRI if available (most sensitive); CT if MRI not available
- Confirmed non-displaced fracture: cast immobilisation typically 8-12 weeks
- Displaced fracture / proximal pole: surgical fixation generally recommended due to AVN risk
- Safety net: clear written instructions — ASB tenderness after FOOSH = treat as fracture pending imaging regardless of X-ray result

### 6. Evidence

- Icon: 🩷
- Badge: db-evid

Scaphoid fracture: classic high-stakes clinical scenario. Key principle: clinical suspicion overrides a negative plain film X-ray in the first 2 weeks. AVN of the proximal pole is a potentially devastating complication. MRI is gold standard for early diagnosis. Outstanding answer uses the retrograde blood supply anatomy to explain WHY this injury is higher risk than other carpal fractures.
