Neurological Patterns, Mimics & Headache

Key takeaways: UL weakness/sensory disturbance is not always C-spine. Sensory normal? Think motor nerve or muscle. Bilateral CTS may be systemic. Pain first then weakness? Think brachial neuritis. Pattern recognition over imaging.
Peripheral Polyneuropathy
  • 2.4% general population; 8% over-55s
  • Polyneuropathy = diffuse; Mononeuropathy = single peripheral nerve
  • Most common cause = compressive peripheral neuropathy (CTS, Guyon's, AIN, ulnar)
  • Non-compressive causes less frequent but important not to miss
General Examination
  • Length-dependent (distal first); symmetrical; reduced/absent reflexes
  • Muscle weakness and atrophy (distal); decreased pinprick/temp/vibration/joint position sense
  • Other clues: cold peripheries, callous formation, distal hair loss
  • Romberg test â€” always include if large-fibre loss suspected
Sensory Fibre Types
  • Large fibre (B12 deficiency, CMT) â€” impaired vibration/proprioception; loss of proprioception = falls and sensory ataxia
  • Small fibre (diabetes, amyloid) â€” preserved vibration but impaired pain/temp; risk of burns and injuries
Patterns (Lehmann 2020)
  • A. Slowly progressive, distal symmetric, predominantly sensory â€” most common
  • B. Slowly progressive, long-standing, muscle wasting
  • C. Subacute onset and/or proximal involvement
  • D. Subacute/rapidly progressive, multifocal, neuropathic pain +/- autonomic
  • E. Sensory ataxic neuropathy
Multifocal Motor Neuropathy (MMN)
  • Acquired autoimmune; motor or motor predominant
  • Chronically progressive asymmetric weakness; distal UL most significantly (80%)
  • Finger extensors over finger flexors; differential finger extension weakness (Slee et al, Neurol 2007)
  • Cold paresis up to 80%; LMN: fasciculations and cramps common
  • Neurophysiology: conduction block; anti-GM1 antibodies (Vlamis et al 2012)
  • Treatment: responsive to IVIg (long-term maintenance); worsens with steroids
MADSAM (Lewis Sumner Syndrome)
  • Bilateral asymmetric hand weakness and sensory loss â€” predominantly ulnar and median nerves
  • Pure sensory or sensory-motor; pain in 22%
  • Probably a type of CIDP; steroids and IVIg effective (Lehman et al JNNP)
PTS â€” Extended Detail
  • Probably incidence 1:1000; M:F 3:2; strong predilection for superior trunk
  • Monophasic course 75%; can involve trapezius, phrenic nerve, recurrent laryngeal nerve
  • Kiloh-Nevin sign (AIN) â€” unable to make OK sign (FPL and FDP paralysis)
  • Scapular winging 70%; NCS: reduced SNAPs; CMAPs usually normal unless severe
  • FVC and CXR if phrenic nerve involvement suspected
  • 70% exhibit pain and functional impairment at 3 years after onset (Candayan 2022)
Hirayama Disease
  • Juvenile onset; unilateral or asymmetric weakness/amyotrophy of hand and ulnar forearm; painless
  • Sparing of brachioradialis; no sensory disturbance; no lower limb involvement
  • Abnormal forward-shifting posterior dura on neck flexion MRI
  • Management: neck collar, surgical decompression â€” improved outcomes
ALS â€” Flail Arm Variant
  • Many misdiagnosed as MMN; proximal predominant; slightly slower than classical ALS
  • Split Hand Sign â€” APB and FDI preferentially affected; relative sparing of ADM (Hardiman 2011; Eisen 2012)
Inclusion Body Myositis
  • Sporadic muscle disease of aging; almost exclusively over 40yr; M:F 2:1
  • Slowly progressive; most are wheelchair dependent 20 years after onset
  • Treatment supportive; misdiagnosis is very common; association with T-cell lymphocytic leukaemia (Nicolau 2020)
Autonomic Neuropathy
  • Orthostatic hypotension; GI symptoms; erectile dysfunction; urinary retention; dry eyes/mouth
  • With neuropathy: think Amyloidosis, Diabetes
